# Exercises · Fundamentals of E-commerce

The case data and examples are fictional.

## 01. Digital commerce: models, channels and who controls what

Ten-business map: teams place ten business cards on a model-by-channel grid and answer the five control questions for each. They close by proposing two channel positions for Molienda and writing, in one line each, what control each one costs.

Form your team and choose the project business (a real micro or small business whose owner agrees to answer questions, your own product, or one of the three backup cases). Submit one page: its current channels, the five control questions for each and one of your own purchases traced through the five jobs.

Assess concept (40%), evidence (40%) and decision (20%). State any additional assumption.

## 02. Customer, value proposition and evidence

Interview clinic in pairs: turn bad questions into good ones and swap with another pair to flag the leading ones. Then design a one-week test of the Molienda subscription with the threshold set in advance, for example at least 20 refundable deposits from 400 visits, and write what you would do if it is not met.

Three real interviews for the project business, findings placed on the evidence ladder and one test proposal with its metric, threshold and end date.

Assess concept (40%), evidence (40%) and decision (20%). State any additional assumption.

## 03. The economics of an order

In a spreadsheet, the P&L of three Molienda baskets: one 250 g bag, order #1001 (two 250 g bags at $289 and one grinder at $1,490, total $2,068 MXN) and one subscription month. The commission, payment and shipping lines are flagged to be filled in w04, w07 and w10, using today's illustrative values for now. Find the basket that loses money, propose one fix (threshold, bundle or price) and test it with numbers.

A cost sheet for three products of the project business, with a source for every number, and the contribution per order of each one.

Assess concept (40%), evidence (40%) and decision (20%). State any additional assumption.

## 04. Platforms: rent, buy or build

Three paths for Molienda: with an illustrative fee table, compute the monthly cost at 50, 300 and 2,000 orders for marketplace only, a SaaS store and open source. Chart the crossover, fill in the commission line of order #1001's P&L and complete the tool card for the chosen option, with a 90-day exit plan.

Put together project Checkpoint 1 (due at the start of w05): control questions, segment and value proposition, evidence on the ladder, the P&L of three products and the platform decision with its tool card and exit plan.

Assess concept (40%), evidence (40%) and decision (20%). State any additional assumption.

## 05. Catalog and product data

Build Molienda's catalog (three roasts, whole bean or ground, in 250 g or 1 kg, plus the grinder and the subscription) as a SKU table with a naming convention and sample GTINs; map one product to two channel taxonomies and fact-check, line by line, an AI-written description with three planted errors.

A 10-SKU catalog for the project business with a naming convention and attributes, plus one product JSON filled in from the template.

Assess concept (40%), evidence (40%) and decision (20%). State any additional assumption.

## 06. The shopping experience, the product page and the funnel

Take apart Molienda's mobile product page (a mock with planted defects) using the given funnel data: heuristic evaluation, an accessibility pass with a checker and the keyboard, and a written decision on which step to fix first and why.

Audit your team's product page (or prototype) and a competitor's: five issues with severity, four accessibility findings and one funnel hypothesis.

Assess concept (40%), evidence (40%) and decision (20%). State any additional assumption.

## 07. Payments: how money moves

Molienda's payment mix: from a segment table (a young buyer without a credit card, an office buyer who needs an invoice, a grinder customer) and an illustrative fee table, choose methods, compute the weighted payment cost per order, fill in the payment line of order #1001, decide whether to offer MSI only on grinders and complete a tool card for one payment provider.

The project's payment mix with a net-received table by method and a tool card for one payment provider.

Assess concept (40%), evidence (40%) and decision (20%). State any additional assumption.

## 08. Checkout, trust and conversion optimization

Molienda's seven-step checkout, with about 6,000 sessions a month: redesign it, write two hypotheses and check with the sample-size table whether an A/B test is feasible. The expected finding is that only large effects are detectable, so a usability test comes first. A short lab because of the exam week.

Midterm 1 practice set (20 mixed items from the w01 to w08 quizzes, self-scored and ungraded) and the team's checkout redesign for Checkpoint 2.

Assess concept (40%), evidence (40%) and decision (20%). State any additional assumption.

## 09. Orders, inventory and risk

The history of order #1001: a shuffled event log with a duplicate webhook and a stock conflict between the store and Mercado Libre. Rebuild the order's history in a trace table, find the oversell and decide what to do with three suspicious orders.

The project's order lifecycle diagram, inventory policy and five fraud rules with the estimated cost of their false positives (part of Checkpoint 2).

Assess concept (40%), evidence (40%) and decision (20%). State any additional assumption.

## 10. Fulfillment and the last mile

For Molienda: choose a fulfillment model at three volumes, compute the billable weight of three boxes, write delivery promises from Coatepec to Mexico City, Guadalajara, Monterrey and Mérida, carry shipping into order #1001's P&L and complete a tool card for one logistics provider.

The project's fulfillment plan and delivery promise, with shipping cost carried into the order P&L.

Assess concept (40%), evidence (40%) and decision (20%). State any additional assumption.

## 11. Returns, after-sales and invoicing

Molienda's after-sales desk handles six cases: a damaged grinder, the wrong grind, a change of mind on day 3, an invoice requested a month later, a refund of an invoiced order and a review complaining of late delivery. For each: the customer's right, the action, the cost and the CFDI document.

The project's returns and warranty policy, after-sales cost estimate and invoicing flow (part of Checkpoint 3).

Assess concept (40%), evidence (40%) and decision (20%). State any additional assumption.

## 12. Acquisition: reaching the right customer

Molienda's campaign table (Meta, Google, Mercado Ads and one creator): compute CAC, ROAS and break-even ROAS, reallocate the budget, tag three links and complete a tool card for one ad channel. It closes with a puzzle left open on purpose: Meta reports 40 purchases, analytics 25 and the store 32. Why? Answered in w13.

The project's acquisition plan: budget, expected CAC, break-even ROAS, UTM convention and an analysis of a competitor's ads in a public ad library.

Assess concept (40%), evidence (40%) and decision (20%). State any additional assumption.

## 13. Measurement and decisions

Explore the GA4 demo account (funnel and traffic acquisition by UTM). Write Molienda's measurement plan and build the reconciliation table that solves the w12 puzzle, including a duplicate purchase event planted in the data. A short lab because of the exam week.

Midterm 2 practice set (mixed items from w09 to w13 plus w03 economics, ungraded) and the team's measurement plan and KPI tree for Checkpoint 3.

Assess concept (40%), evidence (40%) and decision (20%). State any additional assumption.

## 14. Retention and customer value

From a 12-month export of Molienda orders (about 500 rows), build the cohort table, RFM segments and LTV. Then design, on paper, a subscription flow that complies with the law.

The project's retention plan: LTV, segments and three messages with their consent mechanism. This completes Checkpoint 3, due at the start of w15.

Assess concept (40%), evidence (40%) and decision (20%). State any additional assumption.

## 15. Law, privacy and ethics

Molienda legal audit: fifteen screenshots (checkout, cookie banner, subscription flow, product page, privacy notice). Find the violations and deceptive patterns, rewrite each one and draft the elements of the privacy notice.

For the final project report: compliance checklist, draft privacy notice and a deceptive-design self-audit. This is an academic exercise, not legal advice.

Assess concept (40%), evidence (40%) and decision (20%). State any additional assumption.

## 16. Evaluating the next tool

Short lab: each team draws a trend card (checkout inside an assistant, live shopping, marketplace advertising, passkeys, social marketplaces) and produces a tool card and a pilot plan for Molienda. Project presentations follow.

Deliver the final project report. For the final exam: a 300-word reflection on one retired instance, what replaced it and which concept survived.

Assess concept (40%), evidence (40%) and decision (20%). State any additional assumption.
