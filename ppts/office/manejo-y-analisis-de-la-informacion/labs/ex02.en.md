# Exercise 2 · Managing worksheets, named ranges and freeze panes

The student builds this workbook from nothing. There is no file to download: the exercise starts with a blank workbook, grows three sheets, fills one of them with a 31-day calendar, and then names, resizes, deletes, hides and freezes its way through the sheet-management half of week 3, session 5. COBERTURA puts it there next to Exercise 5, and notes that this one already opens both the Series dialog and the Name Manager, which are two of the eight additions that session carries. Hand in the finished workbook as `Exercise 2 Excel_Your name.xlsx`.

**Objectives** MO-200 1.2.2, 1.3.2, 1.4.3, 2.1.2, 2.1.3, 2.3.1; MO-201 2.1.2

A note on scope. Adding, renaming, moving, copying, colouring, hiding and deleting worksheets is what the syllabus calls session 5, and it is not a numbered objective in either MO-200 or MO-201. Those commands are course content, not exam content. Everything else in this exercise is numbered, and the routes below cover it.

## The data

**There is no workbook for this exercise.** The source folder holds only `Excel2_Instructions.docx`. The student types the data, which is 31 numbers and 31 weekday names, and the typing is part of the point: task 6 is where Auto Fill earns its place.

For reference, the finished March (2) sheet looks like this after task 8, before any rows are deleted or hidden. The weekday column starts on Wednesday.

| No. | Day of the week |
|---|---|
| 1 | Wednesday |
| 2 | Thursday |

Only the first two values of each column get typed. Everything below row 3 comes from a fill.

## What to do

1. Create a new workbook. `Ctrl+N`. The instruction sheet gives `Ctrl+U`, which is the Spanish binding for Nuevo; in an English interface `Ctrl+U` is underline.
2. Add two more worksheets after `Sheet1`. Three routes: click the `+` next to the sheet tab, use **Home** > **Cells** > **Insert** > **Insert Sheet**, or press `Shift+F11`.
3. Rename the three sheets `January`, `February` and `March`. Double-click the tab name, or right-click the tab and click **Rename**.
4. Reorder the tabs to `January`, `March`, `February`, then make a copy of `March`. Right-click the tab, click **Move or Copy**, pick the position under **Before sheet**, and select the **Create a copy** check box. Without that check box the sheet moves instead of copying. The copy arrives as `March (2)`.
5. Colour the `January`, `February` and `March` tabs blue and the `March (2)` tab red. Right-click the tab, **Tab Color**. Then hide `March (2)`: right-click the tab, **Hide**.
6. Unhide `March (2)` again, because the next tasks are on it. Right-click any tab, click **Unhide**, select the sheet, click **OK**. The **Unhide** entry is greyed out unless the workbook has a hidden sheet, which is how you can tell task 5 worked.
7. On `March (2)`, put the numbers 1 to 31 down column A and centre them. Type `1` in A1, then use **Fill** > **Series...** with a Step value of 1 and a Stop value of 31. Route 2.1.2 and Expert 2.1.2.
8. In column B, put the day of the week for each row, starting at Wednesday, and align the values left. Type `Wednesday` in B1 and fill down. Weekday names are a built-in custom list, so a fill continues them and wraps at Sunday.
9. Name A1:A31 `Number` and B1:B31 `Day`, using the Name Box at the left end of the formula bar. Route 2.3.1, short route. The Name Box always scopes to Workbook and never shows what it built, which is why task 12 goes back through the Name Manager.
10. Insert one row above row 1, type `No.` in A1 and `Day of the week` in B1, then name A1:B1 `Headers`. Route 2.1.3. Excel shifts the two ranges named in task 9 down by one row on its own, so `Number` still covers the numbers.
11. Autofit both column widths to their content, and set the height of every used row to 21.00, which is 28 pixels. Route 1.3.2. The row height is a number, so it has to be typed into the dialog rather than dragged.
12. Open the **Name Manager** and rename `Number` to `Num`. Route 2.3.1. A name cannot be edited in the Name Box once it exists, which is the reason this task is here.
13. Delete the rows whose weekday is a day this subject meets. Route 2.1.3, **Delete Sheet Rows**.
14. Hide the rows for one of the weekend days. Right-click the row headings and click **Hide**.
15. Freeze the panes so the header row and the `Num` column stay on screen while the sheet scrolls. Select B2 and use **Freeze Panes**, the first entry, not **Freeze Top Row** and not **Freeze First Column**. Route 1.4.3.
16. Save as `Exercise 2 Excel_Your name.xlsx` and upload it to Moodle.

Optional, and the reason task 9 exists: press `F5`, pick `Num` from the **Go to:** list and click **OK**. That is route 1.2.2, which COBERTURA adds to this session precisely because defining a name and navigating to one are two different objectives.

## Exam routes used here

**1.2.2 Navigate to named cells, ranges, or workbook elements.** Press `F5` or `Ctrl+G` to open the **Go To** dialog, or go to the **Home** tab, **Editing** group, click **Find & Select**, then **Go To...**. In the **Go to:** list click the defined name. Every workbook-scoped name appears there; sheet-scoped names appear only while their sheet is active. Click **OK**, and the range is selected rather than merely scrolled to. For a cell with no name, type the address into the **Reference:** box, for example `March (2)!B47`. For workbook elements rather than names, click **Special...** in the same dialog and choose the element type in **Go To Special**: **Comments**, **Constants**, **Formulas** with its **Numbers**, **Text**, **Logicals** and **Errors** sub-boxes, **Blanks**, **Current region**, **Current array**, **Objects**, **Row differences**, **Column differences**, **Precedents**, **Dependents**, **Last cell**, **Visible cells only**, **Conditional formats**, **Data validation**. The Name Box drop-down is the short route and reaches names but no workbook element. `Ctrl+G` then `Alt+S` opens Go To Special in two keystrokes.

**1.3.2 Adjust row height and column width.** Select the rows or columns by clicking their headings, dragging across headings, or `Ctrl`-clicking a non-adjacent set. **Home** tab, **Cells** group, **Format**. For rows click **Row Height...**, type `21` into the **Row height:** box, click **OK**. For columns click **Format** again and **Column Width...**, and note that the unit is characters of the standard font rather than points, which is why 20 is a wide column and 20 is a short row. To size to content instead of to a number, click **Format** and then **AutoFit Row Height** or **AutoFit Column Width**. **Default Width...** changes the sheet default. Dragging the border between two headings is the short route and cannot satisfy a task that names a number, because 21.00 is not a position the mouse can find. Key tips: `Alt, H, O, H` row height, `Alt, H, O, W` column width, `Alt, H, O, A` autofit row height, `Alt, H, O, I` autofit column width.

**1.4.3 Freeze worksheet rows and columns.** Work out the anchor cell first: Excel freezes everything above and everything to the left of the selected cell, so one header row plus one label column means the anchor is B2. Click that single cell, not the row heading and not the column heading. **View** tab, **Window** group, **Freeze Panes**, then **Freeze Panes** in the drop-down. The two entries below it, **Freeze Top Row** and **Freeze First Column**, ignore the selection and freeze exactly one line each. To release, open the same menu and click **Unfreeze Panes**. Key tips `Alt, W, F, F`.

**2.1.2 Fill cells by using Auto Fill.** Type the first value. If the step is not 1, type the second value too and select both. Select the seed cell together with the whole range to be filled. **Home** tab, **Editing** group, **Fill**. For a straight copy, **Down**, **Right**, **Up** or **Left**. For a pattern, **Series...**, then **Series in** as **Rows** or **Columns**, **Type** as **Linear**, **Growth**, **Date** or **AutoFill**, the **Step value**, and a **Stop value** when the task gives an end instead of a range. Click **OK**. Dragging the fill handle is the short route; the **Auto Fill Options** button appears after a drag and does not appear after a ribbon fill, which is the quickest tell of which route was taken. `Ctrl+D` fills down, `Ctrl+R` fills right. Ribbon keys `Alt, H, F, I, S` reach the Series dialog, **TO CONFIRM** on the lab build.

**2.1.3 Insert and delete multiple columns or rows.** Excel inserts exactly as many rows as are selected, so select one whole row heading to insert one row. **Home** tab, **Cells** group, click the **arrow on the Insert button**, not the button face, then **Insert Sheet Rows**. To delete the class-day rows, select those whole rows, click the **arrow on the Delete button**, then **Delete Sheet Rows**. New rows inherit the formatting of the row above; the **Insert Options** paintbrush that appears next to the insertion offers **Clear Formatting** if the task wants them clean. `Ctrl+Shift+plus` inserts and `Ctrl+minus` deletes, and with whole rows selected both act immediately.

**2.3.1 Define a named range.** Select the range. **Formulas** tab, **Defined Names** group, **Define Name**. In the **New Name** dialog type the name in the **Name** box: it starts with a letter, an underscore or a backslash, carries no spaces, and cannot be a cell address such as `A1`. Open **Scope** and pick **Workbook** or a sheet name. Fill **Comment** if the task asks for a description. Check that **Refers to** holds an absolute reference such as `='March (2)'!$A$2:$A$32`; the collapse arrow at the right of the box reselects on the sheet. Click **OK**. To name several ranges from their labels at once, select the block including the header row and use **Create from Selection**, ticking **Top row**, **Left column**, **Bottom row** or **Right column**. To edit or delete, go to **Formulas** > **Defined Names** > **Name Manager** and use **New...**, **Edit...**, **Delete** and **Filter**. Renaming `Number` to `Num` happens here, because the Name Box can create a name and cannot change one. `Ctrl+F3` opens Name Manager, `Ctrl+Shift+F3` opens Create from Selection, `Alt, M, M, D` opens Define Name.

**Expert 2.1.2 Fill cells by using advanced Fill Series options.** Everything the drag cannot do. Type the starting value, select the range that will hold the series beginning with that cell, then **Home** tab, **Editing** group, **Fill**, **Series...**. Under **Series in** pick **Rows** or **Columns** to match the direction of the selection, because Excel guesses and guesses wrong when the selection is a single cell. Under **Type** pick **Linear**, **Growth**, **Date** or **AutoFill**: Linear adds the step, Growth multiplies by it. With Date, the **Date unit** group enables and offers **Day**, **Weekday**, **Month**, **Year**, where Weekday skips Saturday and Sunday. Type the **Step value**, and the **Stop value** when the series has to end at a number rather than at the end of the selection. **Trend** fits a line or a curve through values already in the selection. Click **OK**. The other graded route is the right-drag: hold the right mouse button on the fill handle, drag, release, and pick **Copy Cells**, **Fill Series**, **Fill Formatting Only**, **Fill Without Formatting**, **Fill Days**, **Fill Weekdays**, **Fill Months**, **Fill Years**, **Linear Trend**, **Growth Trend** or **Series...** from the menu. Custom lists live at **File** > **Options** > **Advanced** > **General** > **Edit Custom Lists...**, with **List entries** for typing and **Import list from cells** for pulling a range off the sheet.

## Checks

- The workbook holds four sheets in the order `January`, `March`, `February`, `March (2)`. The first three tabs are blue, the fourth is red.
- Column A of `March (2)` runs 1 to 31 with no gaps, centred, and each cell shows a constant in the formula bar rather than a formula. A filled series never leaves a formula behind.
- Column B starts at Wednesday, left aligned, and wraps back to Monday after each Sunday.
- Open **Name Manager**. Three names: `Num`, `Day`, `Headers`. `Number` is gone. Each **Refers To** is an absolute reference with the sheet name on it, and none of them reads `#REF!`.
- Click the Name Box drop-down and pick `Num`. The whole number column highlights, which proves the range survived the row insert in task 10.
- Reopen **Format** > **Row Height...** with several rows selected. It reads 21 rather than blank. A blank box means the rows differ and the selection missed some.
- No column shows `#####` and no text is clipped, which is what AutoFit Column Width leaves behind.
- Scroll down. Row 1 and column A stay on screen, and a thin solid dark line marks the freeze at the B2 corner. Reopen the **Freeze Panes** menu and the first entry now reads **Unfreeze Panes**, which is the cheapest proof that a freeze and not a split is in effect.
- The hidden weekend rows show as a break in the row numbering, and the deleted class-day rows are gone from the numbering entirely.

## Known problems in the source

- There is no workbook. The source folder holds the instructions and nothing else. That is deliberate here, unlike Exercise 25, where COBERTURA flags the missing file as a cost the week cannot absorb.
- The instruction sheet gives `Ctrl + u` for a new workbook. That is the Spanish binding for Nuevo. In English it is `Ctrl+N`, and `Ctrl+U` is underline.
- Step 5 of the original hides `March (2)` and step 6 then asks the student to work on it. Task 6 above adds the unhide the original leaves implicit.
- The original says "you can use the author fill function", which is a machine translation of autorrelleno. It means Auto Fill.
- The original mentions `Ctrl +` and `Ctrl -` for inserting and removing rows. The insert combination is `Ctrl+Shift+plus`.
