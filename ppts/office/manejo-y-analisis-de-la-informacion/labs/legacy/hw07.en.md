# Homework 7 · IF, IFERROR, sparklines and conditional formatting

Ninety-seven car models with three years of unit sales each, and eleven empty columns to fill beside them. Sales share per year, whether the share went up or down, the percentage change in sales, a text label for the size of that change, a sparkline, a tax rate looked up from a small table, and a two-condition classification. ## What to do

1. Complete the information in the table.
   - **Sales Share**, `K9:M105`. The model's percentage of total sales for that year. Operations with relative references, with the year total held absolute.
   - **Change in share 2020 vs 2019**, `N9:N105`. Return the word `Increase` or the word `Decrease`. IF function.
   - **Change in sales 2020 vs 2019**, `P9:P105`. The percentage change in sales between the two years. Operations with relative references.
   - **Type of variation**, `Q9:Q105`. If the variation was positive, `Increase in Sales`. If the decrease was up to -20 per cent, `Moderate decrease in Sales`. If the decrease is greater than -20 per cent, `Severe decrease in sales`. If the variation shows `N/A`, the type of variation must also read `N/A`. IF function.
   - **Trend**, `R9:R105`. Sparklines over units sold from 2018 to 2020, that is `G:I` of the same row. Route MO-200 2.4.1.
   - **% Tax**, `T9:T105`. The tax rate that corresponds to the row, according to the table provided in `X8:Y12`. IF function.
   - **Classification**, `V9:V105`. `Relevant` if the average sales share of the three years is greater than 10 per cent, or the variation in 2020 is greater than 8 per cent. If neither condition is met, `Not Relevant`. IF with OR.
2. Generate an additional table holding the number of units sold in 2020 by brand, separating national from imported. Use **Remove Duplicates** on the brand column to build the brand list, route MO-201 2.2.5, then SUMIFS for the two counts, route MO-201 3.1.1.
3. Conditionally format the `Type of variation` column: `Severe decrease in sales` in light red, `Moderate decrease in sales` in light yellow, `Increase in sales` in light green. Cells reading `N/A` get no colour. Route MO-200 2.4.2.
4. No error message may appear in any calculation, and no variation may be calculated where the cells hold no data. In both cases the result must read `N/A`. IFERROR.
5. Freeze panes so that the `Origin` column and the table header row stay visible. Route MO-200 1.4.3. `Origin` is column `F` and the header is row 8, so the split goes at `G9`.
6. Set the table from the `Theme` column to the `Classification` column as the print area, that is `A8:V105` or `A1:V107` depending on how much of the banner is wanted. Set printing to 3 pages high by 2 pages wide. Rows 1 through 8 and columns A through F must repeat on every page. Routes MO-200 1.5.1 for the print area and MO-200 1.3.1 for the scaling and the repeating titles.

## Conditional formatting already in the workbook

Three rules ship with the sheet, all three on the single cell `Q106`. `Q106` is the empty row between the last data row, 105, and the total row, 107, in the `Type of variation` column. They are the rules task 3 asks for, applied to one cell instead of to the column, which is what happens when the dialog is opened without selecting the range first.

| Priority | Range | Type | Condition | Fill |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `Q106` | cellIs | equal to `"Crecimiento en Ventas"` | `FFCDF3CD`, light green |
| 2 | `Q106` | cellIs | equal to `"Decremento moderado en Ventas"` | theme accent 2 at 60 per cent lighter, which renders as `F8CBAD`, light orange |
| 3 | `Q106` | cellIs | equal to `"Decremento grave en Ventas"` | `FFFFB3B3`, light red |

Two things about those rules matter for anyone rebuilding them.

The three strings are Spanish, in a workbook whose data, headers and instruction sheet are all English. `Crecimiento en Ventas` is the increase, `Decremento moderado en Ventas` the moderate decrease, `Decremento grave en Ventas` the severe one. A `cellIs` equal rule is an exact string match, so a formula in column `Q` that writes the English labels named in task 1 will never satisfy any of these three rules, and a formula that writes the Spanish labels will not match the English wording of the task. Pick one language for the column and write the three rules to match it exactly, character for character, including the capital V in `Ventas`.

The task text and the rules also disagree on which colour goes with which label. The task says light yellow for the moderate decrease; the rule in the workbook carries an orange fill. The two red and green rules agree with the task.

To rebuild the rules: select `Q9:Q105`, not one cell, then **Home**, **Styles**, **Conditional Formatting**, **Highlight Cells Rules**, **Equal To...**, type the string in the left box, and pick **Custom Format...** on the right to set the fill from the hex values above. Repeat for the other two. The `N/A` cells need no rule of their own, because a cell that matches none of the three conditions keeps its normal fill.

## Checks

The three totals on row 107, which are already in the workbook as formulas, are 40,401 for 2018, 47,156 for 2019 and 31,249 for 2020. Each sales share column must total 100 per cent against its own year total, so `SUM(K9:K105)`, `SUM(L9:L105)` and `SUM(M9:M105)` each come to 1 once the blanks are handled.

Reading the type of variation rule as written, with the moderate band running from 0 down to -20 per cent inclusive:

| Label | Rows |
|---|---|
| Increase in Sales | 16 |
| Moderate decrease in Sales | 8 |
| Severe decrease in sales | 61 |
| N/A | 12 |

Two of those eight moderate rows, sheet rows 41 and 61, are not decreases at all: 2020 equals 2019 exactly. The instructions name a positive variation and two sizes of decrease and never say what a flat year is, so a solution that puts those two rows anywhere is defensible as long as it is consistent. No row lands exactly on -20 per cent, so that boundary never has to be decided.

The twelve `N/A` rows break down as six rows with no 2019 figure, sheet rows 19, 33, 78, 79, 81 and 83; four rows with no 2020 figure, rows 16, 101, 103 and 104; and two rows where 2019 is zero and the percentage change would divide by zero, rows 29 and 44. Row 19 has no 2018 figure either. If any cell in column `P` or `Q` shows `#DIV/0!` or `#VALUE!`, task 4 has not been done.

The five rows where 2020 sales are zero, rows 11, 51, 70, 95 and 96, are a real -100 per cent and belong in `Severe decrease in sales`, not in `N/A`. A solution that blanks them has confused an empty cell with a zero.

The sparkline column must hold 97 sparklines, one per row, each reading three cells. Sparklines sit in the cell background, so a sparkline that has landed in the wrong cell is easiest to spot by clicking through column `R` and reading the **Data Range** box on the **Sparkline** tab.

## Notes on the source

- The Tareas folder holds no homework 10. The set runs 1 to 9 and then jumps to 11. Nothing has been renumbered here to close the gap.
- The instruction sheet names the required file `Excel_Hmw7 (students)`. The workbook in the folder is `Hmw7_Excel(students).xlsx`.
- The tax lookup table cannot be used with a single lookup. Its four keys are `National Auto, Luxury Segment`, `National Car, High Segment`, `Imported Car, Luxury Segment` and `Imported Auto, High Segment`. Two of them say `Auto` and two say `Car`, and neither word appears in the data, where the two facts live in separate columns as `National` or `Imported` and `Luxury` or `High`. So no concatenation of the two data columns will match all four keys. This is why the instruction sheet lists the IF function against this row rather than VLOOKUP: the four rates have to be reached with nested IFs on `C` and `F`. The table is a legend, not a lookup range.
- The fourth key, `National Car, High Segment` at 14 per cent, matches no row in the data. There are no national cars in the high segment.
- Columns `A` and `B` hold the same value on all 97 rows, `Vehicles sales to public` and `Cars`. They carry no information and exist only to make the sheet look like an extract from a larger report.
- The instruction sheet writes the three variation labels with different capitalisation in different places, `Increase in Sales` and `Moderate decrease in Sales` with a capital S, `Severe decrease in sales` without. The conditional formatting rules in the workbook use Spanish strings and so match none of them. Whichever spelling is chosen has to be identical in the formula and in the rule.
- The instruction for task 4 says variations should not be calculated "if there is no data in the cells" and gives IFERROR as the tool. IFERROR does not catch an empty cell; an empty divisor produces `#DIV/0!` and is caught, but an empty numerator quietly returns -100 per cent. Catching the empty cells needs an explicit test, for example `=IF(OR(H9="",I9=""),"N/A",IFERROR((I9-H9)/H9,"N/A"))`.
- Task 6 asks for rows 1 through 8 to repeat on every page. Row 8 is the header row and rows 3, 4, 5 and 7 are empty, so this is the banner plus the header, which is what a repeating title block should be.
- `A2` reads `Segment:  Luxury Cars` but 17 of the 97 rows are in the high segment, not the luxury one. The banner describes only part of the sheet.
