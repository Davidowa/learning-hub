# Legacy exercise 05 · Operators and wildcards

Three sheets of pure logic. A block of eighteen number rows where every answer is a comparison, a sixteen row truth table where every answer is AND, OR or NOT over the same four criteria, and a list of twenty three Mexican place names counted nine different ways with COUNTIF and wildcards. Nothing is added up and nothing is formatted: every answer cell returns `TRUE`, `FALSE`, or a count. It comes from a previous year of the course, where it was exercise 5, and the current syllabus records no slot for it. The instruction sheet names the workbook to open, `5.1 Excel 5 (alumnos)_Operadores`, and never says what is handed in.

**Objectives** MO-201 3.1.1. The comparison half of the exercise has no objective of its own in either exam, and is the groundwork under MO-200 4.2.3.

## What to do

**On `OPERADORES`.**

1. Fill D2:I19. Each column evaluates the expression written in its own header for the X, Y and Z of that row, and the result can only be `TRUE` or `FALSE`. The six formulas for row 2, filled down to row 19, are `=A2=B2`, `=A2+B2>B2+C2`, `=A2*B2<A2*C2`, `=(A2+B2)/C2>=(B2+50)/35`, `=A2<=50` and `=A2<>(B2+C2)/2`. Every reference is relative, so one fill down does all eighteen rows.

**On `AND-OR-NOT`.**

2. Fill E2:K17, one operator per column, over the criteria the header names, again returning only `TRUE` or `FALSE`. Column E is AND over all four criteria, F is OR over all four, G is NOT over all four, H is AND over criteria 1, 2 and 3, I is OR over criteria 1, 2 and 3, J is AND over criteria 2 and 4, and K is OR over criteria 2 and 4. Route MO-201 3.1.1. The shapes for row 2 are `=AND(A2:D2)`, `=OR(A2:D2)`, `=NOT(AND(A2:D2))`, `=AND(A2:C2)`, `=OR(A2:C2)`, `=AND(B2,D2)` and `=OR(B2,D2)`. NOT takes a single argument, so column G has to be read as NOT applied to the same all four combination, and the source does not say which combination that is.

**On `COMODINES`.**

3. Count, with COUNTIF and wildcards, how many of the twenty three names in A2:A24 meet each of the nine conditions in C2:C10, writing each count beside its condition. The conditions, translated, are: starts with `Tlal`, starts with `Tla`, ends with `co`, second letter is `a`, second to last letter is `a`, ends with `pan`, contains an `x` anywhere in the name, contains a `z` anywhere in the name, and third letter is `a`. Route MO-201 3.1.1. The shape is `=COUNTIF($A$2:$A$24,"Tlal*")`, where `*` stands for any run of characters, including none, and `?` stands for exactly one character. The workbook has no answer column, so the counts go in D2:D10 unless the instructor says otherwise.

## Notes on the source

- The instruction document for this exercise, `Excel_Ejercicio5.docx`, is written entirely in Spanish, and it is the only one of the previous years instruction files that is. The tasks above are translated. The sheet names, the column headers and the nine conditions are left in Spanish because they are the text the workbook actually holds.
- The Spanish function names in the original are CONTAR.SI for COUNTIF, Y for AND, O for OR and NO for NOT, and the results it asks for are VERDADERO and FALSO, which are `TRUE` and `FALSE`.
- The document calls the second sheet `AND-OR-NO` while the workbook names it `AND-OR-NOT`.
- The document lists the operators practised as `=`, `>`, `<` and `<>`, but the column headers on `OPERADORES` also use `>=` and `<=`.
- Header I1 on `AND-OR-NOT` is `O 1, 2, 3, ` with a trailing comma and a trailing space, and J1 is `y 2,4` in lower case where every other header is upper case.
- `NO(TODO)` is the one column the source leaves genuinely open, because NOT takes a single argument and the header names four criteria. It is read here as NOT of the AND over all four.
- `COMODINES` has a heading for the conditions but no heading and no column for the answers, and the used range of the sheet stops at column C.
- COUNTIF ignores case but not accents, and both facts change a count. `Cuautitlán`, `Tultitlán` and `Tepoztán` end in a stressed `á`, which is a different character from `a`, so none of the three matches `"*a?"` and that count is 7 rather than 10. Case going the other way, `Xochimilco` and `Xola` match `"*x*"` on a capital `X`.
- Nothing in the workbook carries conditional formatting, data validation, sheet protection or a chart. All three sheets are plain values with empty answer columns.
