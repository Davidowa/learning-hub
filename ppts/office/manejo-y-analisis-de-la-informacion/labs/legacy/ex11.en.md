# Legacy exercise 11 · Advanced filters

Fifteen advanced filters over the same twenty-seven employees, one criteria range and one extracted result block each, all of them written below the source data. This is the previous years' version of what the current set teaches as [exercise 24](../ex24.en.md). The employee list is the same twenty-seven rows, value for value, and the fifteen questions are the same fifteen questions. What this copy carries that the current one does not is five worked answers: filters 11 to 15 are already built in the workbook, criteria and extracts and all. The student hands in the workbook with all fifteen blocks in place, starting at `B32`.

**Objectives** MO-200 3.3.1, MO-200 4.2.1, MO-201 2.2.5

Advanced Filter is not a numbered objective on either exam. It reaches the exam through MO-201 2.2.5, where **Unique records only** in the same dialog is the graded route for removing duplicates, and through MO-200 3.3.1, which does with menus what a criteria range does by hand. Filter 15 needs AVERAGE, which is MO-200 4.2.1. The click paths for the **Advanced Filter** dialog are written out in full in [exercise 24](../ex24.en.md) and are not repeated here.

## What to do

Everything goes below the source data. The instructions say to start at `B32`; the shipped blocks start one row lower, at `B33`. Give each filter its own band of rows, criteria on top and extract underneath, with blank rows between the blocks, because Advanced Filter reads the whole block surrounding the cell it is pointed at and two blocks that touch become one block.

The workbook supplies criteria text for filters 11 to 15 only. The criteria named below for the other ten are the reading worked out in [exercise 24](../ex24.en.md), not strings from the source.

1. Employees who work in the Centro branch.
2. Employees who work in the Administration department or in the Logistics department.
3. Employees who work in the Accounting department of the Centro branch, or in the Logistics department of the Azcapotzalco branch. Two headings, two rows, four cells. The data spells the branch `Azcapozalco`; see the notes at the end.
4. Employees who work in the Accounting department of the Centro or Tlalpan branches. The department heading is repeated on both rows.
5. Employees whose code begins with the letter E and who were born in 1994. Code `E*`, plus `DOB` twice on the same row for the year band.
6. Employees who do not work in the Azcapotzalco branch and whose salary is less than 6,000. Branch `<>Azcapozalco` and Salary `<6000`, side by side on one row.
7. Employees who do not work in the Administration department or in the Marketing department, and who were born between 1991 and 1994. Department twice, `<>Administration` and `<>Marketing`, plus `DOB` twice for the year band, four headings on one row. The source does not say whether the two end years are inside the band; the count in Checks reads them as inclusive.
8. Employees who work either in the Tlalpan or Tlaltenango branch. Show only name, first and second last names and branch. The four output columns are chosen by typing those headings in the **Copy to** row before the dialog runs.
9. Employees whose first surname begins with the letter `R` and whose salary is between 4,000 and 6,000. Show only the code, the department, and the branch office.
10. Employees whose code contains the letter `A`. Criterion `*A*`.
11. Employees whose code contains the letter `E`. The result should show only the code, first name, last name and branch. Criterion `*E*`, as in the shipped block.
12. Employees whose code begins with the letter `E`. Same four output columns. Criterion `E*`.
13. Employees whose code has as a second character the letter `E`. Same four output columns. Criterion `?E*`.
14. Employees whose code's last character is 8. The shipped block uses a computed criterion, `=RIGHT(B3,1)="8"` written under a heading of `f(x)`, and the heading has to be something that is not one of the ten field names for a computed criterion to work at all. A plain `*8` under the `Code` heading reaches the same four rows.
15. Employees whose salary is higher than the average of the list. This one cannot use a field heading either. The shipped block writes `=J3>AVERAGE($J$3:$J$29)` under `f(x)`. The `J3` stays relative so it walks down the records; the AVERAGE range is locked so it does not move with them.

The five blocks shipped in the workbook match rows 11 to 15 of that table exactly, extract for extract, so a student who rebuilds them and gets a different count has a criteria range problem and not a data problem.

Filter 9 returns the headings and nothing under them. That is the right answer for this data. Four employees have a first surname starting with R and every one of them falls outside the band: Rodriguez at 3,435.77, Rubio at 3,531.08, Ramirez at 6,130.64 and Ruiz at 7,425.17.

Filters 12 and 13 have to disagree. Seven codes start with E, four carry E in second place, and no code is in both lists. If the two return the same rows, the `?` in `?E*` was typed as something else.

Filters 10 and 11 have to overlap without matching: thirteen codes hold an A, seventeen hold an E. If either returns all twenty-seven, the criterion lost a `*` and became "begins with".

Filter 15 returning twenty-seven rows, or one, means the AVERAGE range was left relative and drifted down the sheet as Excel walked the records.

At the end, `B2:K29` still holds twenty-seven rows and none of them are hidden. **Copy to another location** never touches the source, so a source that has lost rows means somebody sorted or deleted inside a result block.

## Notes on the source

- **Azcapotzalco against Azcapozalco.** The instructions spell the borough correctly, Azcapotzalco. The data spells it `Azcapozalco`, without the t, in all five rows that use it. A criterion typed the correct way returns nothing, which is a silent failure in filters 3 and 6. Fix the data or type the criterion to match it, and decide which before writing those two filters.
- **The workbook gives away a third of the exercise.** Filters 11 to 15 ship worked. Anyone handing this file to students should clear rows 33 to 115 first, or hand out the first twenty-seven data rows of the CSV instead.
- **The .docx list arrives without its numbers.** The fifteen questions come out of Word as a tab-indented list whose numbering did not survive extraction. The numbering used here comes from the workbook, whose own labels 11 to 15 sit against the eleventh to fifteenth questions in the list and pin the sequence.
- **The workbook restates the questions in its own words, with a typo.** Its labels read `Personel whose code contains the letter "E". The results should show only the code, name, last name and branch office` where the .docx reads `Employees whose code contains the letter "E". The result should show only the code, first name, last name and branch.` Same question, two wordings, and `Personel` for personnel in all five.
- **Three loose criteria beside block 13.** `K77`, `K78` and `K79` hold `*E?`, `*E*` and `E*`. They belong to no criteria range and nothing reads them. Delete them, or keep them as a record of somebody working out where the wildcard goes.
- **Filter 9 has no answer.** The question is well formed and the data cannot satisfy it. Either keep it as a lesson in reading an empty extract, or widen the band to 3,000 to 7,000, which returns Rodriguez and Rubio.
- **Two names carry trailing spaces**, `Contreras ` on JU-EUT49 and `Flores ` on ED-OOC60. Both are preserved in the CSV. Nothing here filters on second surname, so nothing breaks, and anything that did would break on them.
- **One code breaks the pattern.** Every code is two letters, a hyphen, three letters and two digits, except `DI-UAS-32`, which carries an extra hyphen. It changes none of the fifteen answers, and it would change any criterion built on character positions past the sixth.
- **Bonus is typed, not calculated.** Column K holds five per cent of column J as hard values. Nothing in this exercise reads it, and a student who edits a salary will watch the bonus stay put.
- **The required-files line names a file that does not exist.** The .docx asks for `1 1.1 Excel 11 (students) Advanced filters`; the folder holds `11.1 Excel 11 (students) Advanced filters.xlsx`. The leading `1` and the space after it are a list marker that flattened into the filename.
