# Legacy homework 4 · Written operations practice

Previous years' take-home on turning a written formula into a worksheet formula. One sheet, eighteen paired observations of an hourly wage and the hours worked at it, and six descriptive statistics to calculate. The point is not the statistics; it is that each one arrives as mathematical notation printed above the answer cell, and the student has to recognise which Excel function, or which arrangement of SUM and arithmetic, produces it. The workbook is `Hmw4 Excel (students)_Writing Operations practice.xlsx`, and it is the only one of the five with no instruction document; everything it asks sits on the sheet itself.

**Objectives** MO-200 4.2.1, MO-200 4.1.1. Most of this sheet has no exam route at all; see the notes at the end.

### The six formulas

Row 10 carries six pictures, one under each heading, each one a printed formula. They are Windows metafiles and do not survive conversion to text, so they are written out here in words. This is the only surviving copy of what they said.

| Cell | Heading | What the picture shows |
|---|---|---|
| D10 | Mean | `x̄ = (Σ xi) / n` |
| E10 | Weighted mean | `x̄ = (Σ wi·xi) / (Σ wi)` |
| F10 | Geometric mean | `x̄g = the n-th root of (x1)(x2)…(xn)` |
| G10 | Variance | `s² = Σ(xi − x̄)² / (n − 1)` |
| H10 | Standard deviation | `s = √(s²)` |
| I10 | Asymmetry Coefficient | `Skewness = [ n / ((n−1)(n−2)) ] · Σ [ (xi − x̄) / s ]³` |

The variance uses `n − 1` in the denominator, so the sample forms are the ones intended throughout, and the skewness formula is the sample skewness that Excel's SKEW returns.

## What to do

1. Calculate the mean of the hourly wage. `=AVERAGE(A11:A28)`, or `=SUM(A11:A28)/COUNT(A11:A28)` if the written formula is to be followed literally. Route MO-200 4.2.1.
2. Calculate the weighted mean, each wage weighted by its hours. `=SUMPRODUCT(A11:A28,B11:B28)/SUM(B11:B28)`.
3. Calculate the geometric mean. `=GEOMEAN(A11:A28)`, or the literal reading of the picture, `=PRODUCT(A11:A28)^(1/COUNT(A11:A28))`.
4. Calculate the variance. `=VAR.S(A11:A28)`, or the literal reading, `=SUMPRODUCT((A11:A28-AVERAGE(A11:A28))^2)/(COUNT(A11:A28)-1)`.
5. Calculate the standard deviation as the square root of the variance. `=SQRT(G11)` pointed at the variance answer, or `=STDEV.S(A11:A28)`.
6. Calculate the asymmetry coefficient. `=SKEW(A11:A28)`.

Where the six answers go is not stated. The borders on the sheet box `D9:I10`, a heading row and one row beneath it, which says the answers belong in `D10:I10`; but the six pictures are anchored in exactly those cells and cover them, so a value typed there is invisible until the picture is moved. The row below, `D11:I11`, is unbordered but clear and lines up with the first observation. Pick one and say which before the work is set. If any of the six answers is built out of the mean or the standard deviation rather than from a single function, lock that reference before copying it anywhere, route MO-200 4.1.1.

## Checks

There are 18 observations. `SUM(A11:A28)` is `370`, `SUM(B11:B28)` is `5637`, and `SUMPRODUCT(A11:A28,B11:B28)` is `114636`. Those three intermediate values catch a range that stopped a row short before any of the statistics are read.

| Statistic | Value |
|---|---|
| Mean | 20.5556 |
| Weighted mean | 20.3363 |
| Geometric mean | 20.3793 |
| Variance | 7.7908 |
| Standard deviation | 2.7912 |
| Asymmetry coefficient | 0.3353 |

The standard deviation squared must equal the variance exactly, since one is defined from the other. The weighted mean comes out slightly below the plain mean, which is the sheet telling you that the lower wages were worked for more hours; if a weighted mean comes back identical to the plain mean, the weights were not applied.

If the population forms are used by mistake, the variance reads `7.3580` and the standard deviation `2.7126`. Both are wrong for this sheet, because the printed formula divides by `n − 1`.

## Notes on the source

- Six `.emf` images were embedded in this workbook, anchored in `D10` to `I10`, one under each of the six statistic headings. They are the written formulas the exercise is named for, and they are lost with the workbook: `.emf` is a Windows metafile format that carries no text layer, and nothing in this repository can hold it. Their content was read by rendering each one and is transcribed in the table above, which is now the only record of it. The pictures themselves are gone.
- There is no `Homework4 Excel_Instructions.docx`. The `Tareas` folder has instruction documents for homework 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 and 11, and none for 4 or 10. The instructions on the sheet itself are all there ever was, and they say what to calculate but not where to put it or which function to use.
- The sheet name is `CÁLCULOS`, in Spanish, while every word of content on it is in English.
- Almost nothing here is examinable. AVERAGE and SUM are MO-200 4.2.1 and absolute references are MO-200 4.1.1, but GEOMEAN, VAR.S, STDEV.S, SKEW, SUMPRODUCT and PRODUCT appear on neither the MO-200 nor the MO-201 objective list. This is a statistics exercise wearing a spreadsheet, not spreadsheet practice, and it is worth knowing that before scheduling it against exam preparation.
- The sheet ships with no answers and no named ranges, so it is usable as it stands. What formatting there is comes to bold headings on `A1`, `D9:I9` and `A10:B10`, a light fill on those two heading blocks, and borders around `D9:I10` and `A10:B28`. Nothing is formatted as a number, and the answers will need a decimal format of their own.
- A small empty text box sits over `G17`. It holds nothing and has no bearing on the exercise.
