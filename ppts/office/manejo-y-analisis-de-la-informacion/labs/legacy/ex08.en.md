# Exercise 08 · Lookups and AutoFilter

Two halves that share a workbook and nothing else. The first half is a hardware inventory of 2,583 items copied out three times, once the ordinary way, once with the key column moved off the left edge, and once laid on its side, so that the same question has to be answered with three different lookup functions. The second half is a year of appliance sales that arrives with no formatting at all, and has to be formatted, extended with four calculated columns, marked up by hand and by rule, and then filtered six ways. Hand in the workbook with the three answer tables filled on `Inventory`, and with the formats, the four new columns, the two conditional formats, the hand-coloured cells and the six filter results on `Products`.

**Objectives** MO-200 1.3.2, MO-200 2.2.2, MO-200 2.2.4, MO-200 2.2.5, MO-200 2.2.6, MO-200 2.3.1, MO-200 2.4.2, MO-200 3.3.1, MO-200 3.3.2, MO-200 4.1.1, MO-201 2.3.1, MO-201 3.2.1

## What to do

**On the `Inventory` sheet.**

1. Name the Inventory 1 database `Base1`. Route MO-200 2.3.1, **Formulas** tab, **Defined Names** group, **Define Name**, or the Name Box.
2. Name the Inventory 2 database `Base2`, by the same route.
3. Name the Inventory 3 database `Base3`, by the same route.
4. Complete Table1 with formulas that pull the description, the unit of measurement and the cost out of the product listing, using VLOOKUP. Route MO-201 3.2.1.
5. Complete Table2 the same way, using XLOOKUP. The instruction sheet flags why: the search range is not in the first column of Inventory 2, so VLOOKUP cannot reach it from the item number.
6. Complete Table3 the same way, using HLOOKUP. The instruction sheet flags why: Inventory 3 presents the information horizontally.

**On the `Products` sheet.**

7. Apply these five formats to the database. Routes MO-200 1.3.2 for the widths, MO-200 2.2.5 for the number formats, MO-200 2.2.2, 2.2.4 and 2.2.6 for the headings and the borders.
   - Auto-fit the width of the columns.
   - Date format on the `Sale Date` column, `A2:A409`.
   - Comma Style on `Unit cost` and `Unit selling price`, `H2:I409`.
   - Green fill, wrapped text, centred alignment, and a white bold font on the headings, `A1:J1`.
   - Simple borders across the whole database.
8. Create four new columns, `Total Cost`, `Total Sales`, `Total Profit` and `% Profit`, and fill all four with formulas rather than typed numbers. Route MO-200 4.1.1. They land in `K` to `N`, since `J` is the last column in use.
9. On the `Total Sales` column apply a conditional format using a three-arrow icon set. Route MO-200 2.4.2, **Conditional Formatting**, **Icon Sets**, a three-arrow set from the **Directional** group.
10. On the `% Profit` column apply a three-colour traffic light icon set, red below 30 per cent, yellow from 30 per cent to 40 per cent, and green above 40 per cent. Route MO-201 2.3.1, because the thresholds have to be typed: **New Rule...**, **Format all cells based on their values**, **Format Style** set to **Icon Set**, the three traffic lights chosen, and the two boundary values entered as `0.4` and `0.3` with **Type** set to **Number**, or as `40` and `30` with **Type** set to **Percent**. Reverse the icon order if the green light comes out at the bottom.
11. In the `Product` column pick five cells at random and give them a yellow fill. Route MO-200 2.2.6.
12. In the same `Product` column pick five other cells at random and set their font colour to red, then five more and set their font colour to blue. Same route.
13. Insert filters into the database and find the following six results. Route MO-200 3.3.1 for the first five, and MO-200 3.3.2 for the ordering in the sixth.
    - The dates in the first quarter of 2018.
    - The dates in September 2018.
    - The `Product` column filtered by colour.
    - The `Brand` column showing only the records that begin with `PH`.
    - The `Total Sales` column filtered by icon.
    - The ten highest sales, ordered from highest to lowest.

## Notes on the source

- Item `485501480` in `G8` and `Q8` does not exist anywhere in Inventory 1 or Inventory 2, so the fourth row of Table1 and of Table2 can only return `#N/A`. Item `450812524` in `X14` does not exist in Inventory 3 either, and the nearest number in the block is `450812520`, so the fourth row of Table3 also returns `#N/A`. Whether these are deliberate error cases or three typos cannot be told from the file. Teach them as the error case, or correct the three cells before handing the workbook out.
- The instruction sheet lists the objective as "Functionis: VLOOKUP, HLOOKUP, VLOOKUP". The typo in the first word aside, the third entry is wrong: the body of the same sheet asks for XLOOKUP on Table2, and XLOOKUP is what the transposed key column requires.
- XLOOKUP is not named in either MO-200 or MO-201, so `procedures.en.md` carries no click path for it. MO-201 3.2.1 covers VLOOKUP, HLOOKUP, MATCH and INDEX only. INDEX with MATCH solves Table2 by a graded route if the class needs one.
- The instruction sheet names three columns that the sheet does not have. `SalesDate` is `Sale Date`, `Unit Price` is `Unit selling price`, and the `Mark` column is `Brand`. `Mark` is a translation of the Spanish `Marca`, and reading it as `Brand` is the only reading that makes "records that begin with PH" return anything.
- "Total Sale column" in tasks 9 and 13 is `Total Sales`, one of the four columns task 8 asks the student to create. Those two tasks cannot be done before task 8.
- The three answer tables ship with the defined names `Table1`, `Table2` and `Table3` already in place. Those names are not asked for anywhere in the instructions, and the three names the instructions do ask for, `Base1`, `Base2` and `Base3`, are not in the file. Naming a plain range `Table1` is also a poor habit to model, because it reads as a table name and no table exists here.
- The instruction sheet never says how far the three databases reach. Read as complete blocks with their header rows, `Base1` is `B3:E2586`, `Base2` is `L3:O2586` and `Base3` is `V3:AJ6`.
- Descriptions in all three inventory copies use non-breaking spaces between words. This is invisible on screen and matters twice: a retyped description will not match, and text filters on that column behave differently from what a student expects.
- The `Sale Date` column holds real dates but carries the General format, so the sheet opens showing five-digit serial numbers. That is what makes the first formatting task necessary, and it is worth saying out loud rather than letting students think the column is broken.
- The data is billed as a year of 2018 sales, and 406 of the 408 records are. Two records are dated 1 January 2019. A first-quarter filter written as "on or after 1 Jan 2018 and on or before 31 Mar 2018" is unaffected; a filter written as "the whole year" picks up two rows that do not belong to it.
- `Brand` holds `PHILIPS` 122 times and `Philips` once, in row 408. Excel's text filters ignore case, so "begins with PH" catches all 131 records including the eight `PHILCO`, but a formula written with EXACT would not.
- The traffic light thresholds in task 10 are given as percentages of profit without saying profit over what. Profit over sales is the only reading that puts records in all three bands, so that is the reading used above. Profit over cost puts all 408 records above 42 per cent.
- `Category` in column `D` is in Spanish while every other value on the sheet is in English. The exercise never reads it.
- The `Products` half of this exercise survives in the current course as Exercise 15, which uses the same 408 sales and the same six filters.
