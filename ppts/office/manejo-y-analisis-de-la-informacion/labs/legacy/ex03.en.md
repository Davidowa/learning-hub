# Legacy exercise 3 · The curve, the rounding family and the Analysis sheet

The third of four exercises on one workbook, and the long one. The first half finishes the grade
columns: a curve rule written with IF, the three rounding functions used on three different columns so
the difference between them shows, a truncation, a sparkline per student and an age worked out of the
date of birth. The second half builds a new sheet called `Analysis` and fills it with counts,
conditional averages and four charts. There is no file of its own; it opens what legacy exercise 2
saved, and legacy exercise 4 prints what this one produces.

**Objectives** MO-200 2.2.2, MO-200 2.2.5, MO-200 2.2.6, MO-200 2.4.1, MO-200 4.1.1,
MO-200 4.1.2, MO-200 4.2.1, MO-200 4.2.2, MO-200 4.2.3, MO-200 5.1.1, MO-200 5.2.3, MO-201 2.2.5,
MO-201 3.1.1, MO-201 3.3.1.
ROUND, ROUNDUP, ROUNDDOWN, TRUNC, YEAR and ISERROR are named in the objective list of the source and
have no objective code of their own in either exam.

## The data

None of its own. This exercise works on the workbook carried out of legacy exercise 2, whose sheets
and CSV exports are described in [ex01.en.md](ex01.en.md), and whose homework figures come from the
table in [ex02.en.md](ex02.en.md). The `Analysis` sheet does not exist in the shipped workbook; task 9
is where it gets created, empty.

Column letters below are the ones in force after the two columns inserted in exercise 1, task 9. The
map is in [ex02.en.md](ex02.en.md).

## What to do

1. Apply the curve. The rule as the source states it: if the laboratory grade is below 6, the final
   grade is `7.0`. Write it in `T` with IF, against the lab grade in `R`, keeping the fixed cells
   fixed and the relative ones relative. Route MO-200 4.2.3 and MO-200 4.1.1. What the formula returns
   when the lab grade is 6 or more is not stated anywhere in the document; see the notes.
2. Round the theory grade up, the practice grade down, and the final grade the ordinary way. Three
   different functions on three columns: ROUNDUP, ROUNDDOWN and ROUND.
3. Centre everything. Route MO-200 2.2.2, and the source asks for the other alignment options to be
   explained at the same time.
4. Put percentage formatting on the top cells, meaning the weight row. Route MO-200 2.2.5. It shows
   `0.25` as `25%` without changing the value the formulas read.
5. Truncate the curve to two decimal places with TRUNC, and since it is the final grade, give it a
   different and larger font. Route MO-200 2.2.6. TRUNC cuts the digits off rather than rounding, so
   this is not the same as task 2 or the display format from exercise 1, task 17.
6. Add a sparkline of each student's grades. Route MO-200 2.4.1. The source does not name the range.
   A student's grades sit in `M` to `P`, with the lab grade apart from them in `R`.
7. Calculate each student's age in years from the date of birth added in legacy exercise 2, using
   TODAY, YEAR and a subtraction of dates. Route MO-201 3.3.1.
8. Calculate the average age, and the maximum and the minimum. Route MO-200 4.2.1.

Everything from here goes on a new sheet named `Analysis`.

9. List the programs, one entry each, with UNIQUE or with Remove Duplicates. Route MO-201 2.2.5.
10. Against each program, the number of students on it and its share of the whole as a percentage,
    with COUNTIF and the range names defined in legacy exercise 2. Routes MO-201 3.1.1 and
    MO-200 4.1.2.
11. Count how many students have paid for the laboratory and how many have not, with COUNTA,
    COUNTBLANK and ISBLANK. Route MO-200 4.2.2. Read the note below before grading this one.
12. Count the male and the female students, with COUNTIF. Route MO-201 3.1.1.
13. Average grade per program, with AVERAGEIF. Route MO-201 3.1.1.
14. Average grade per state, counting only students aged 20 or over, with AVERAGEIFS, and clear any
    error message the calculation produces with ISERROR. Route MO-201 3.1.1; ISERROR has no objective
    code of its own.
15. Count the women and the men in each state, with COUNTIFS. Route MO-201 3.1.1.
16. Build four charts. Routes MO-200 5.1.1 and MO-200 5.2.3.
    - a pie chart of the ratio of men to women,
    - a column chart of the number of students per program,
    - a line chart of the grades,
    - a chart of the number of students by state, whose type the source does not name.

## Notes on the source

- Task 1 states one branch of the curve and not the other. "If the laboratory grade is less than 6,
  the final grade will be 7.0" says what happens below 6 and says nothing about what happens at 6 or
  above, and the phrase "grade with a curve" is never defined anywhere in the four documents. The most
  likely reading is that the other branch keeps the `Theory and Lab average`, which is what the
  averages in the last column of the check table above assume, but that is a reading and not the
  source.
- Task 2 says "theory grade up, practice grade down". The sheet has no column called practice; `Lab
  grade` is the only candidate. It holds whole and half numbers apart from two values, `5.9` and
  `5.2`, so rounding it down moves the 11 rows ending in `.5` and those two.
- Task 11 asks for COUNTA, COUNTBLANK and ISBLANK on a column whose 20 empty-looking cells hold empty
  text rather than nothing at all. The three functions do not treat that the same way, so the task can
  produce three different answers on the same column depending on which one is used. Check the
  behaviour on the lab build before grading, and consider clearing the column with Delete first.
- Task 14 asks for error messages to be corrected, but at any date in the 2020s no state returns one:
  every state has at least one student and every student is over 20, so no AVERAGEIFS in the table
  divides by zero. The error the task expects only appears if the age threshold is raised or if the
  state list is written by hand and includes a state nobody comes from.
- The list of programs in task 9 will hold both spellings from the two original sheets, `Marcadotecnia`
  and `Merca`, unless the replace in legacy exercise 1, task 14 was extended to them, which the source
  does not ask for.
- Task 16 names three chart types and leaves the fourth as "a graph". It also does not say which
  grades the line chart is to plot.
- UNIQUE is a dynamic array function and is not available in older builds of Excel, which is why the
  source offers Remove Duplicates as the alternative in the same breath.
