# Legacy homework 5 · IF with AND and OR

Previous years' take-home on combining conditions. Two exercises. The first is a matchmaking sheet: pick five attributes from drop-down lists, then work out which of ten friends match all five and which match at least one. The second is a travel agency awarding a prize on either of two conditions. It is the only one of the five workbooks that carries data validation, a hidden sheet and sheet protection, so this file is also the only record of those once the `.xlsx` is gone. The workbook is `Hmw5_Excel(students).xlsx` and the wording comes from `Homework5 Excel_Instructions.docx`, whose title reads "Exercise IF with Y/O", `Y` and `O` being the Spanish names of the AND and OR functions.

**Objectives** MO-200 4.2.3, MO-200 4.2.2, MO-200 4.1.1, MO-201 3.1.1, MO-201 2.2.2, MO-201 1.2.2

## What to do

### Exercise 1

1. On `Result`, choose the option you prefer from the drop-down list on each attribute, `B7` through `B11`. Route MO-201 2.2.2 for how the lists were built.
2. In the yellow cells of `Friends`, `G2:K11`, write a formula per attribute that says whether that friend matches the corresponding choice on `Result`. Each formula compares one cell of `B2:F11` against one cell of `Result!B7:B11`, so lock the reference to `Result` before filling, route MO-200 4.1.1.
3. In `L2:L11`, the `And (all)` column, combine the five checks so the cell is true only when the friend matches every attribute. Route MO-201 3.1.1, AND inside IF.
4. In `M2:M11`, the `Or (all)` column, combine them so the cell is true when the friend matches at least one. Route MO-201 3.1.1, OR inside IF.
5. On `Result`, in the yellow cell `A14`, count the friends who meet all the characteristics, from the `And (all)` column. COUNTIF over `Friends!L2:L11` against whatever the column returns. Route MO-200 4.2.2.
6. In the yellow cell `A19`, count the friends who meet at least one, from the `Or (all)` column, the same way.

The instruction document names `A13` and `A18` for those two counts. Both of those cells hold the sentence that introduces the answer, and the empty yellow cells directly beneath them are `A14` and `A19`. The yellow fill is the reliable guide.

### Exercise 2

7. On `Exercise2`, complete `D5:D13` so the cell reads `Trip to Cancun` when either condition holds: more than 25 units sold, or a `STATUS` of `Paid`. One IF over an OR does it, `=IF(OR(B5>25,C5="Paid"),"Trip to Cancun","")`. Routes MO-200 4.2.3 and MO-201 3.1.1.
8. In `B16`, count the sellers who made no sales, `=COUNTIF(B5:B13,0)`.
9. In `B17`, count the sellers who won the prize, `=COUNTIF(D5:D13,"Trip to Cancun")`.
10. Format the information table appropriately.

## Notes on the source

- The workbook is broken as shipped. The drop-down lists on `Result` are in English and the data on `Friends` is in Spanish, so a direct comparison such as `=Friends!B2=Result!$B$7` returns FALSE on every row, forever. The lists translate to the data in the order they are written: `Blue` is `Azul`, `Light brown` is `Café claro`, `Dark brown` is `Café obscuro`, `Gray` is `Gris`, `Green` is `Verde`; `Short` is `Bajo`, `Regular` is `Media`, `Tall` is `Alto`; and `Yes` is `Si`. Either translate the `Friends` data into English, retype the three validation lists in Spanish, or put a lookup between the two. Nothing in the instructions acknowledges the problem.
- The hidden `Características` sheet holds exactly those lists in Spanish, in the same order as the English ones on `Result`. It is almost certainly what the validation used to point at before someone retyped the lists in English, which is how the mismatch was introduced. It is now referenced by nothing.
- The password on `Características` protects a five-row list of eye colours. There is no reason for it, and it will stop a student from unhiding and reading the sheet that would explain the mismatch above.
- The instruction document points at `A13` and `A18` on `Result` for the two counts, and at `B14` and `B15` on `Exercise2` for the two totals. All four are wrong. The answer cells are `A14`, `A19`, `B16` and `B17`, identified by the fill colour and by which cells are empty.
- The age criterion is never defined. `B9` holds `22` and the instruction says only to indicate which friends fulfil what she is looking for. Exactly 22 is the reading used above and gives one match, Gustavo. Read as 22 or older it gives five, and the whole `Or (all)` count moves from 9 to 10 because Ranfo joins at 23. Decide before marking.
- `Result!A2` reads "My friend Denisse asks me for support to introduce her friends. result, I decide who to present him to." A clause is missing in the middle and the pronoun is wrong at the end. The instruction document has the intact version: "My friend Denisse asks me to help her to meet new friends. I ask her what she is looking for and from this result, I decide who to introduce to her."
- The `Friends` sheet gives seven answer columns, `G` through `M`, but only names five of them in the instructions. `L` and `M`, `And (all)` and `Or (all)`, are the two summary columns and are what the counts on `Result` read.
- No sheet in the workbook is solved, and there is no conditional formatting and no chart anywhere in it.
