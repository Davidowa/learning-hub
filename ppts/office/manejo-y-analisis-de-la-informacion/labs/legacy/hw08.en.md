# Homework 8 · Text to Columns, HLOOKUP, data validation and sparklines

A five-year income statement that arrives as fifteen lines of comma-separated text crammed into a single column. Splitting it is the first task and everything else depends on it. Once it is a real table, it gets named, gets a summary block that pulls four figures for one chosen year with HLOOKUP, gets a dropdown so the year can only be one of the five that exist, and gets a column of sparklines beside it. It is the only homework in the legacy set that starts from unparsed text. Hand in the finished workbook.

**Objectives** MO-200 2.3.1, MO-200 2.2.5, MO-200 2.4.1, MO-201 2.2.2, MO-201 3.2.1

## What to do

1. Change the format of the information, separating the text into columns. **Data** tab, **Data Tools** group, **Text to Columns**. Choose **Delimited**, set the delimiter to **Comma**, and check that the preview shows six columns before finishing. There is nothing in column `B` onwards, so nothing is overwritten.
2. Format the information, treating the amounts as figures of money. Route MO-200 2.2.5. The five year columns, `B3:F15`, are the amounts. Row 2 holds the years and must stay plain numbers, not currency and not thousands-separated, because the HLOOKUP in task 4 matches against them.
3. Assign a name to the data array. The instruction sheet says to use the Name Box; route MO-200 2.3.1 covers the same result through **Formulas**, **Defined Names**, **Define Name**, which is the route that shows the reference it built before committing. The range to name is the whole table including the year row, `A2:F15`, because HLOOKUP needs the year row to be the first row of its table array.
4. Create a summary showing the following four figures, for one given year only:
   - Net Income
   - Gross Profit
   - Operating Profit
   - Consolidated Profit

   Use HLOOKUP against the named range, with the chosen year in a single cell that all four formulas point at. Route MO-201 3.2.1.
5. In the column following the year 2020, that is column `G`, insert Column sparklines, with the highest point and the lowest point highlighted. Route MO-200 2.4.1. The **Sparkline** tab that appears once a sparkline is selected carries the **High Point** and **Low Point** checkboxes in the **Show** group.
6. Display a list holding the years on which the summary in task 4 can be generated, so the year cell cannot be typed freely. **Data** tab, **Data Tools** group, **Data Validation**, **Allow** set to **List**, with **Source** pointing at `B2:F2`. Route MO-201 2.2.2.

The split worked if every cell from `B3` to `F15` right-aligns on its own, which is Excel saying it read a number. A column that stays left-aligned was split into text, usually because a stray character rode along with the field.

The statement is internally consistent, which gives four independent ways to prove the split lost nothing. In each of the five year columns:

- Gross profit equals net revenues minus cost of sales.
- Operating profit equals gross profit minus selling expenses minus administrative expenses plus other income minus other expenses.
- Earnings before taxes equals operating profit plus interest income minus interest expenses.
- Net profit equals earnings before taxes minus income taxes.

The four HLOOKUP row indices, counting from row 2 of the named range as row 1, are 2 for net revenues, 4 for gross profit, 9 for operating profit and 14 for net profit. Change the year cell from 2016 to 2020 and all four summary figures must change together; three of the four turn negative.

The validation list must offer exactly five entries, 2016 to 2020, and must refuse 2021. Test it by typing 2021 into the year cell and confirming that the error alert appears.

Column `G` holds thirteen sparklines, one per line item on rows 3 to 15, each reading `B:F` of its own row, with the tallest and the shortest bar in a different colour from the rest.

## Notes on the source

- The Tareas folder holds no homework 10. The set runs 1 to 9 and then jumps to 11. Nothing has been renumbered here to close the gap.
- The instruction sheet names the required file `Excel_Hmw8 (students)`. The workbook in the folder is `Hmw8_Excel(students).xlsx`.
- Two of the four summary labels do not match any row label in the data. `Net Income` has to mean `Net revenues`, the top line, and `Consolidated Profit` has to mean `Net profit`, the bottom line. Read literally, `Net Income` is the bottom line and `Consolidated Profit` is not an item on this statement at all, which would make two of the four lookups return the same row. The reading used in the checks above takes them as the first and last rows of the statement, which is the only one that gives four distinct answers.
- The instruction sheet's activity table is headed `Función o commando` in Spanish, with `commando` misspelled, while the rest of the sheet is English. The same header appears in homework 9 as `Function or command`.
- The delimiter in the source cells is a comma followed by a space. Text to Columns splitting on the comma alone puts a leading space in front of every number, which Excel still parses as a number, so the split appears to work. It does not appear to work on the label column, where the leading space survives into the text and breaks any later lookup that matches a label exactly. Ticking **Space** as a second delimiter is the wrong fix, because it also splits `Cost of sales` into three columns. The clean route is to split on the comma and then trim, or to set **Treat consecutive delimiters as one** off and use the **Advanced** button only for the decimal settings.
- The instruction sheet lists sparklines under the objective but never says which rows get them. Task 5 only says the column following 2020. The thirteen line-item rows are the reading used here; the title row and the year row have nothing to plot.
- No year in the statement is labelled as the current one, and the 2020 column is the only one where cost of sales exceeds net revenues. That is the shape of the data, not an error.
