# Exercise 10 · Charts and tables

Four unrelated data sets, thirteen tasks, and ten of them are charts. A web portal's access log wants five different chart types off one block of data, a stress questionnaire wants random answers, validated input, a lookup and a chart whose label reads a cell, fifty years of Mexican census figures want a comparison, a line chart and a map, and a clothing distributor's order book wants random dates, a lookup, data bars, a real Excel table and a paste into it. Hand in the workbook with the charts built on `Exercise1`, `Exercise2` and `Exercise3`, and with `Ejercicio4` filled, formatted, converted to a table and extended by the eight orders from `Ej 4-2`.

**Objectives** MO-200 2.4.2, MO-200 3.1.1, MO-200 3.2.1, MO-200 4.2.1, MO-200 5.1.1, MO-200 5.2.1, MO-200 5.2.2, MO-200 5.2.3, MO-200 5.3.1, MO-201 2.2.2, MO-201 2.3.1, MO-201 3.2.1, MO-201 4.1.1, MO-201 4.1.2, MO-210 2.1.5

## Checks

- `Exercise1`, chart 1 has five bars and their heights read 473, 84, 291, 949 and 1264. If the tallest bar is 41877, the chart is plotting `Total` and not `Active`.
- Chart 4 must show two value axes, because a time of day is stored as a fraction between 0 and 1, here between 0.60 and 0.80, and cannot share a scale with an email count that reaches 522. If the time series is invisible, it was left on the same axis. Four of the twenty-one days have no sending time, so that series has four gaps.
- Chart 5 compares two totals, 1017 for Company A over the twenty-one days and 1204 for Company B, which shows as 45.8 per cent against 54.2 per cent once the percentage data labels are switched on.
- `Exercise2`, `D15:D35` holds twenty-one whole numbers, each between 1 and 7. `D37` is between 1 and 7 as well; it cannot be outside that range whatever the random draw. Typing `8` into any of the twenty-one cells raises the error alert, and clicking one of them shows the input message.
- `Exercise3`, chart 11 has five series and seven categories, or seven series and five categories depending on the orientation chosen. Distrito Federal is the only one of the five that falls between 1980 and 1990, from 8,831,079 to 8,235,744, so its line or its bar group has a visible dip.
- Chart 13, the map, must resolve thirty-two Mexican states. Three of the names carry a trailing space and Excel may fail to place them; if three states come out blank, trim the names before rebuilding the chart.
- `Ejercicio4`, every one of the twenty-two dates in `C3:C24` falls between 1 January and 30 June 2021, so `D3:D24` only ever holds 1 through 6, and the data bars only ever span six values.
- `H3:H24` comes out as eight `Sent` for NUEVO LEON, eight `In progress` for GUANAJUATO, three `Delivered` for VERACRUZ, and three `Payment pending`, two for SINALOA and one for DURANGO. That is twenty-two. After the paste in task 19 the counts are nine, eleven, five and five over thirty orders.
- After task 19 the table holds thirty rows of data and the table range reads `B2:H32`. The eight pasted orders arrive with no date and no month, so their `Order Month` cells stay empty and their status formulas fill down on their own if the paste landed inside the table rather than under it.

## Notes on the source

- The instruction sheet lists the required file as `10.1 Excel 10 (students)`. The file in the folder is `10.1 Charts and Tables (students).xlsx`.
- The fourth sheet is named `Ejercicio4` while the first three are `Exercise1`, `Exercise2` and `Exercise3`. The fifth is `Ej 4-2`, with a space, while the instruction sheet calls it `Ej4-2`.
- Task 5 asks for "an area chart (pie chart) with 3D effect". Those are two different chart types. Everything else in the sentence, comparing two companies and showing the percentage of each, only makes sense for a pie, so it is read as a 3-D pie here. An area chart of two daily series is a defensible second reading and produces no percentages.
- The scale table on `Exercise2` sits with the labels in `G` and the numbers in `H`, so VLOOKUP cannot search it: the average in `D37` is a number and the number column is the second one. Three ways out, none of them written down in the source. Swap the two columns and use VLOOKUP with `TRUE` for an approximate match. Use `=XLOOKUP(D37,H8:H14,G8:G14,,-1)`. Or use INDEX with MATCH. The instruction sheet says only "VLOOKUP", which as written cannot work.
- Task 9 also collides with the four sentences at `B40` to `B49`, which describe bands of 2 to 3, 3 to 4, above 4, and exactly 5. Those bands do not line up with the seven-point scale in `G8:H14`, and nothing in the file says which of the two the answer in `G37` should come from.
- The stress questionnaire mixes fifteen negative statements with six positive ones, `Having a good day`, `Being happy`, `Feeling optimistic` and `Feeling energetic` among them. A questionnaire like this normally reverse-scores those before averaging. The exercise asks for a plain average and nothing else, so the average it produces is not a stress score in any real sense.
- `Zacatecas` in 1995 reads 13,336,496 in `G36`. Every other figure for that state sits between 665,524 and 1,353,610, and the state's real 1995 count was about 1,336,496. The extra digit makes Zacatecas the largest state in the country for one census, which will dominate any chart drawn from column `G`, and it is the single most likely thing to confuse a student building task 12.
- Task 11 names `Estado de México`. The sheet calls that state `México` in `A19`, which sits three rows away from the country name in the title. There is no separate row for the country.
- The years in `B4:H4` are stored as text, so a chart built from the block treats them as labels rather than as numbers. That is what the exercise wants here, but it also means the six censuses are spaced evenly on the axis when they are not evenly spaced in time: five decades and then a five-year gap to 1995.
- The `Color` column of the status table on `Ejercicio4` spells the last entry `REd`. Both `SINALOA` and `DURANGO` carry the status `Payment pending`, so the two rows are meant to hold the same colour and one of them is a typo.
- Two origins in the order book look like data entry slips: `PLANTA NORTE C` in `F22`, where every other value is one of six named plants, and `DORADO` in `F24`, which is not a plant at all. Nothing in the exercise reads the origin column, so neither breaks anything.
- The order numbers sort as text, so `2021-10` follows `2021-1` and `2021-2` lands between `2021-19` and `2021-20`. Orders `2021-3` and `2021-30` onwards are the ones held back on `Ej 4-2`.
- MONTH, used in task 14, is not named in either MO-200 or MO-201, so `procedures.en.md` carries no click path for it. Neither is RANDBETWEEN, which ex07 handles by citing MO-210 2.1.5 and reaching the function through **Insert Function**.
- Tasks 6 and 14 both generate random values that change on every recalculation. Nothing in the source says to freeze them, and every chart and every lookup built on top of them will move each time the sheet recalculates. Pasting the generated values back over themselves as values, the way ex07 does, is the fix.
