# Homework 9 · Filters, validation and formatting on a supermarket inventory

A supermarket stocks medicines, personal care products and food across three warehouses, and wants three questions answered off one sheet: the whole list sorted by type and description, the three items with the most money sitting idle, and the medicines running below fifty units. Getting there means adding an ID column, restricting what can be typed into two of the fields, calculating VAT conditionally, formatting the sheet, and then copying it twice so each filtered answer keeps its own sheet. Hand in the workbook with three sheets, `Data`, `Filter` and `Filter 2`.

**Objectives** MO-200 2.1.2, MO-200 2.1.3, MO-200 2.2.5, MO-200 2.2.6, MO-200 2.2.2, MO-200 2.2.4, MO-200 3.3.1, MO-200 3.3.2, MO-200 4.2.3, MO-201 2.2.2

## What to do

The three things the supermarket wants to be able to see, quoted from the instruction sheet, are the targets the filters have to hit:

- **A.** The information organised by type and in ascending alphabetical order by description.
- **B.** The three items whose inventory in money is the largest, and therefore cost the most to have sitting idle.
- **C.** The items with stocks of fewer than 50 units that are medicines.

The steps:

1. Add a column at the beginning of the data table, before the `WAREHOUSE NUM` column. Name it `ID`. It holds a consecutive whole number starting at 1. Routes MO-200 2.1.3 for the insert and MO-200 2.1.2 for the fill; the instruction sheet lists the Name Box and Autofill against this row.
2. Format the `UNIT COST`, `VAT` and `TOTAL COST` fields as currency. Route MO-200 2.2.5.
3. Format `QUANTITY IN STOCK` as a whole number with a thousands separator. Restrict what may be entered in that column: it cannot be an amount less than zero. Include an input message and an error message. Routes MO-200 2.2.5 and MO-201 2.2.2. In **Data Validation**, **Allow** set to **Whole number**, **Data** set to **greater than or equal to**, **Minimum** 0, then fill the **Input Message** and **Error Alert** tabs.
4. The `TAXABLE` field may hold only `Yes` or `No`. Use a validation so nothing else can be entered, with an input message and an error message. **Allow** set to **List**, **Source** typed as `Yes,No`. Then assign values to the cells in this column at random, some `Yes` and some `No`.
5. Calculate `VAT` only where `TAXABLE` equals `YES`. The formula is `QUANTITY IN STOCK * UNIT COST * 0.16`. Route MO-200 4.2.3.
6. `TOTAL COST` is `QUANTITY IN STOCK * UNIT COST + VAT`. Relative references throughout.
7. Format the sheet. Headings yellow, bold, centred, with text wrapping. Alternating grey and white rows, with black borders. Every text field centred. Routes MO-200 2.2.6 for the fill and the borders, MO-200 2.2.2 for the alignment, MO-200 2.2.4 for the wrap.
8. Select the list and sort it as target A asks, by type of item and then by description. Route MO-200 3.3.2. **Data** tab, **Sort & Filter** group, **Sort**, with `TYPE OF ITEM` as the first level and `PRODUCT DESCRIPTION` as the second, both ascending.
9. Rename the sheet `Data`.
10. Create a new sheet, copy the information from `Data` into it, and name it `Filter`. Using **Data**, **Filter**, filter the information to answer target B. Route MO-200 3.3.1.
11. For target C, make a copy of the `Data` sheet and name it `Filter 2`. Filter it to answer target C.

## Notes on the source

- The Tareas folder holds no homework 10. The set runs 1 to 9 and then jumps to 11. Nothing has been renumbered here to close the gap.
- The instruction sheet's title reads `HOMERWORK 9`, with the letters transposed.
- It names the required file `Excel_Hmw9(students)_Filters.xls`, with the old `.xls` extension. The file in the folder is `Hmw9_Excel(students)_Filters.xlsx`.
- The opening narrative lists eight fields the sheet is supposed to hold: product key, product description, quantity in stock, minimum required in inventory, price, type of item, unit cost and total cost. The sheet holds warehouse number, type of item, product description, unit cost, quantity in stock, taxable, VAT and total cost. Three of the listed fields, product key, minimum required in inventory and price, do not exist on the sheet, and three of the sheet's fields, warehouse number, taxable and VAT, are not in the narrative. Task 1's `ID` column is the closest thing to the missing product key. Nothing in the numbered tasks depends on the fields that are missing, so the narrative can be read as a description of the general problem rather than of this file.
- Task 4 says the field may hold `Yes` or `No`, and task 5 tests for `YES` in capitals. Excel's `=` comparison is not case sensitive, so `="YES"` matches a cell holding `Yes` and the mismatch is harmless here. It is still worth settling on one spelling, because a validation list typed as `Yes,No` and a filter later typed as `YES` will look inconsistent in the rules manager.
- The sheet name is `Hoja 1`, in Spanish and with a space, in a workbook whose contents are English. Task 9 renames it to `Data`, so this disappears in the finished file.
- Task 10 says to create a new sheet and copy the information into it, while task 11 says to copy the `Data` sheet. Copying the sheet is the better route for both, since it carries the formats, the validations and the column widths along with the values, and a plain paste does not.
- The instruction sheet lists `Data > Order` against the sorting step. The English command is **Sort**; `Order` is a literal rendering of the Spanish `Ordenar`.
- Eleven rows is small for a filtering exercise. Target B in particular, the three largest values, can be read off the screen without a filter at all, so the point of the task is the route rather than the answer.
