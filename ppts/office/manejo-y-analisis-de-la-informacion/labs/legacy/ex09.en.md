# Exercise 09 · Inspection, properties, protection and views

A personnel file of 114 employees that is used as a pretext for everything around the data rather than the data itself. The workbook gets inspected and stripped of the previous owner's name, given properties of its own, extended by one calculated column that is then locked and hidden from the formula bar, protected in two more places, fitted to a printed page, opened twice side by side, and finally the Quick Access toolbar gets one new button. One formula in the whole exercise. Hand in the workbook with column J filled, the protection applied, the page setup fitted, and the properties written.

**Objectives** MO-200 1.3.1, MO-200 1.4.1, MO-200 1.4.2, MO-200 1.4.4, MO-200 1.4.5, MO-200 1.5.4, MO-201 1.2.2, MO-201 3.3.1

### What the workbook already carries

These matter because half the exercise is about finding them.

- An AutoFilter is already applied over `A1:I115`.
- The sheet is already displayed in **Page Break Preview** at 60 per cent zoom, so the view task starts from where the file opens.
- The workbook was saved with two windows open, so the second half of the window task may already look done.
- Defined names: `BD` refers to `=Personnel!$A$1:$I$115`. A sixth, `_FilterDatabase`, is the hidden name Excel maintains for the AutoFilter and is not the student's business.

## What to do

1. Inspect the document. Route MO-200 1.5.4, **File**, **Info**, **Check for Issues**, **Inspect Document**. Three things follow from the report.
   - Determine whether there are hidden rows or columns. There is one hidden column and no hidden rows.
   - Remove all personal information.
2. Add a `Title`, a `Company` name, and your own name as the `Author`. Route MO-200 1.4.5, **File**, **Info**, the properties panel on the right, or **Show All Properties** to reach `Company`. Do this after the inspection, not before, because removing personal information clears the Author field.
3. In column `J` calculate the age of each employee from the date of birth in column `I`. The instruction sheet names YEAR as the function to use. Route MO-201 3.3.1 for TODAY, which is what supplies the current year.
4. Protect the formula in column `J` and hide it from the formula bar. Route MO-201 1.2.2. Hiding a formula is the **Hidden** check box on the **Protection** tab of **Format Cells**, and like locking it does nothing until the sheet is protected.
5. Protect the cells holding the salaries, column `G`. Same route.
6. Protect the worksheet. Same route, **Review** tab, **Protect** group, **Protect Sheet**.
7. Using Page Break Preview, set the printout to two pages long and one page wide. Route MO-200 1.4.2 for the view and MO-200 1.3.1 for the page setup, **Page Layout** tab, **Scale to Fit** group, **Width** set to `1 page` and **Height** set to `2 pages`.
8. Open a new window and set the two windows side by side. Route MO-200 1.4.4, **View** tab, **Window** group, **New Window**, then **Arrange All** and the **Vertical** option.
9. Add the **Print Preview** command to the Quick Access toolbar. Route MO-200 1.4.1.

## Checks

- The Document Inspector, run a second time, reports nothing under **Document Properties and Personal Information**. Under **Hidden Rows and Columns** it reports one hidden column before you unhide it, and nothing afterwards. Note that the inspector reports hidden columns, it does not unhide them for you; that is a separate decision the instruction sheet leaves open.
- **File**, **Info** shows your name in **Author** and the Title and Company you typed. If the Author reverts to blank, the properties were written before the inspection rather than after.
- `J2` reads something on the shape of `=YEAR(TODAY())-YEAR(I2)`, filled down to `J115`. The ages it produces move with the calendar year, so there is no fixed list of correct answers. What must hold is the spread: the oldest employee was born on 24 August 1937 and the youngest on 9 October 1970, so the column always covers a range of exactly 33 years, and the difference between the largest and smallest value in `J2:J115` is 33.
- Click any cell in `J2:J115` with the sheet protected. The formula bar is empty and the status bar refuses the edit. If the formula is visible in the formula bar, **Hidden** was not selected on the **Protection** tab.
- Try to type in `G2`. Excel refuses with the message about the sheet being protected. Try to type in `E2`. It also refuses, because every cell on a new sheet is locked by default and protecting the sheet locks all of them at once. Tasks 4 and 5 only add value if the rest of the sheet is unlocked first, which the instruction sheet does not say to do.
- In Page Break Preview the blue page boundary lines enclose exactly two pages stacked vertically. **Page Layout**, **Scale to Fit** reads **Width** `1 page` and **Height** `2 pages`.
- The title bar of each window reads the file name followed by `:1` and `:2`, and both are visible at once after **Arrange All**, **Vertical**.
- The Quick Access toolbar shows the Print Preview icon, a sheet with a magnifier.

## Notes on the source

- The instruction sheet lists the required file as `9.1 Excel 9 (students) Inspection, protection and view`. The file in the folder is `9.1 Excel 9 (inspection, properties).xlsx`. Same workbook, two names.
- Task 4 says to protect and hide the formula in column `J`, but column `J` is empty until task 3 is done, and the tasks are listed in that order on the sheet, so the order works. Task 3 says only "Formula YEAR" without giving the expression, so the exact formula is not recorded anywhere in the source.
- The instruction sheet never says to unlock the rest of the sheet, so tasks 4, 5 and 6 as written produce a fully locked sheet, and the two specific protections are indistinguishable from the general one. The only visible difference is the hidden formula from task 4. If the point is to show selective protection, the sheet has to be selected in full and unlocked before columns `G` and `J` are locked again.
- The workbook ships in Page Break Preview already, so task 7 begins where it is supposed to end up. Switch to **Normal** first if the class needs to practise the change of view.
- The workbook also ships with two saved window views, so task 8 may open as though it were already done. **New Window** still adds a third.
- Columns `D` and `F` are both about classification and their headers collide. `Categoría` in `D` is the job title, in Spanish, and `Category` in `F` is the piece of office equipment assigned to the employee. Neither is used by any task in this exercise, which is presumably why the collision was never noticed.
- Five defined names in the file point at `#REF!` and are dead. They come from an advanced filter exercise that was built on this data and then removed. **Name Manager** will show them, and they can be deleted without affecting anything. Two of the five, `CRITERIOO` and `CRITERIOY`, are also in Spanish, which is what gives away where they came from.
- The `ID` column is not a key. Eight numbers appear twice, so the 114 employees carry only 106 distinct IDs. Nothing in this exercise looks anything up by ID, but the column cannot be reused as a lookup key without cleaning first.
- The names in columns `B` and `C` are English and clearly synthetic, and at least one row has them the wrong way round: row 4 reads `Constance` as the last name and `Burt` as the first.
- Ages calculated as `YEAR(TODAY())-YEAR(I2)` are wrong by one for anyone whose birthday has not yet passed this year. The instruction sheet asks for YEAR, so that error is baked into the expected answer. `=DATEDIF(I2,TODAY(),"y")` is the correct calculation, and DATEDIF is in neither exam objective list.
