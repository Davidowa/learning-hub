# Legacy homework 2 · Absolute and relative references

Previous years' take-home on what happens to a reference when a formula is filled. Three sheets, each one built around a small block of constants sitting away from the data: two exchange rates, two transport fares, and a card fee with a cash discount. Every answer column is a fill, and every fill crosses a constant that has to stop moving. The workbook is `Hmw2_Excel(students)_Absolute&Relative.xlsx`. The instruction document is one sentence long, so the task list below is read off the sheets' own labels; see the notes at the end.

**Objectives** MO-200 4.1.1, MO-200 4.2.1, MO-200 2.2.5

## What to do

The whole of the instruction document reads: complete the information in each sheet using formulas, and where needed there should be absolute references. Everything below is that sentence applied to the three layouts.

### Exercise1

1. In `C9:C11` convert each peso amount into US dollars by dividing it by the dollar rate in `B3`, with the rate held absolute so the fill does not walk off it, for example `=B9/$B$3`.
2. In `D9:D11` do the same against the euro rate in `B4`, for example `=B9/$B$4`.
3. In `B17:B19` convert each dollar amount into pesos by multiplying it by the dollar rate, for example `=A17*$B$3`.
4. In `E17:E19` convert each euro amount into pesos against the euro rate, for example `=D17*$B$4`.
5. Format the results as currency with 2 decimal places, matching the accounting format the input cells already carry. Route MO-200 2.2.5.

### Exercise2

6. In `F5:F7` calculate the total cost per week for each agency, from the four per-day and per-rental figures in `B` to `E`. Read as seven days of hotel, meals and trips plus one rental of the diving equipment, that is `=(B5+D5+E5)*7+C5`; see the notes at the end for why that reading is not certain.
7. In `G5:G7` add the bus fare in `B10` to the weekly total, with the fare held absolute, `=F5+$B$10`.
8. In `H5:H7` add the plane fare in `B11` in the same way, `=F5+$B$11`.

### Exercise3

9. In `C8:C14` calculate the card commission in money, the price multiplied by the rate in `B3` held absolute, `=B8*$B$3`.
10. In `D8:D14` calculate the cash discount in money against the rate in `B4`, `=B8*$B$4`.
11. In `E8:E14` calculate the final price paid by card, the price plus the commission, `=B8+C8`.
12. In `F8:F14` calculate the final price paid in cash, the price minus the discount, `=B8-D8`.

## Notes on the source

- `Homework2 Excel_Instructions.docx` carries a title, an objective, the file name and one instruction: "Complete the information in each sheet, using formulas. Where needed, there should be absolute references." It never says which cells, which arithmetic, or which references are the absolute ones. Every numbered task above is inferred from the column headings on the sheets, and a student working from the original document alone had to do the same inference.
- `Exercise2` is genuinely ambiguous. Three of the four cost columns say "per day" and the fourth, `Diving equipment rent`, does not, which is why task 6 reads it as one rental for the week. If the intent was that everything is per day, the weekly totals become `3360`, `3290` and `3220`, and the bus and plane columns follow. Nothing in the source settles it.
- `Exercise3` spells commission as `Comission` in `A3` and `C7`. Both are in the workbook, not introduced here.
- The three sheets ship completely unsolved, which is unusual in this collection and makes them usable as they are. The formatting is already done, though: bold headers, coloured fills, borders around the tables, accounting and percentage number formats, and widened columns. Nothing on the sheets is asked of the student except formulas, which is why task 5 is the only formatting instruction above and even that is only to match what is already there.
- No named ranges are defined in the workbook, so absolute references are the only tool available for holding the constants still. That is almost certainly the point of the exercise.
- The exchange rates are undated. `20.46` pesos to the dollar and `19.95` to the euro put the file somewhere around 2020, and the euro rate being below the dollar rate is unusual enough that it may be a typing error in the original.
