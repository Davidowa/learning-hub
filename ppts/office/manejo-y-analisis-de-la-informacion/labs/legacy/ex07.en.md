# Legacy exercise 07 · Text and date functions

Sixty students, the same sixty on both sheets, and eighteen columns to build out of their four fields. One sheet joins and recases names, builds an email and a sentence, and cuts the first ten characters of a tax ID out of the pieces. The other takes the date of birth apart three times over, once with DAY, MONTH and YEAR, once with TEXT, and once with nothing but a custom number format. It comes from a previous year of the course, where it was exercise 7, and the current syllabus records no slot for it. The instruction sheet names the workbook to open, `7.1 Excel 7 (students) Text`, and never says what is handed in.

**Objectives** MO-200 4.3.3, MO-200 4.3.2, MO-200 4.3.1, MO-201 2.2.1. PROPER, TEXT, DAY, MONTH and YEAR are all named in the function glossary but carry no exam objective of their own, so four of the eighteen tasks have no route behind them.

## The data

**Sheet `Text`**, sixty students in B3:F62 under a header row on row 2 and a merged banner in B1:F1 reading `LOCAL STUDENTS`. The input columns are B `ID`, C `NAME`, D `FATHER'S LAST NAME`, E `MOTHER'S LAST NAME` and F `DATE OF BIRTH`. The IDs run `024300` to `024359` and are stored as text, so the leading zero survives. Every name is in lower case. The dates of birth fall in 2000 and 2001 and are stored as real dates under the number format `dd-mmm-yyyy`. Columns G to O are the answer columns and are empty in the shipped file.

## What to do

**On the `Text` sheet**, filling rows 3 to 62 of each column.

1. In G build the full name out of the three name fields with CONCAT. Route MO-200 4.3.3.
2. In H rewrite that name in capitals with UPPER. Route MO-200 4.3.2.
3. In I write the name with only its first letters capitalised, with PROPER.
4. In J build the email with CONCAT. The source does not give the address pattern, so the pieces to join and the domain have to come from the instructor.
5. In K extract the name of the day of the week of the date of birth, with TEXT and the day name code.
6. In L extract the number of the day, with TEXT and the day number code.
7. In M extract the name of the month, with TEXT and the month name code.
8. In N build the sentence `X's birthday is on X of X`, joining with `&` rather than with a function. The source writes all three blanks as X and never says which fields fill them; the reading that matches the columns already built is the name, then the day number, then the month name.
9. In O build the first ten characters of the RFC with concatenation, UPPER, LEFT and TEXT. Routes MO-200 4.3.1 and MO-200 4.3.3. The source names the functions and not the rule, so see the notes on the source before grading this one.

**On the `Dates` sheet**, filling rows 3 to 62 of each column.

10. In G extract the number of the day from the date of birth with DAY.
11. In H extract the number of the month with MONTH.
12. In I extract the number of the year with YEAR.
13. In J produce the day of birth as text, with TEXT and the day number code.
14. In K produce the name of the month of birth as text, with TEXT and the month name code.
15. In L produce the year of birth as text, with TEXT and the four digit year code.
16. In M show only the day of birth, using a custom number format and no formula. Route MO-201 2.2.1, **Format Cells**, **Number** tab, **Custom**, with the code typed into the **Type** box.
17. In N show the name of the month of birth, again with a custom format.
18. In O show the year of birth, again with a custom format.

## Notes on the source

- The workbook ships unsolved. Columns G to O are empty on both sheets, so nothing has to be cleared before the file is handed to students.
- The instruction sheet gives no email pattern for task 4, only the function to use. It is the one task in the file that cannot be graded from the source alone.
- The RFC rule is not stated either. The functions the instruction sheet lists, concatenation with UPPER, LEFT and TEXT, only reach the shape used in the check above: the first two letters of the paternal surname, the first letter of the maternal surname, the first letter of the name, then the date of birth as six digits. The real Mexican RFC takes the first letter and the first internal vowel of the paternal surname and strips accents, which for `lópez` gives `LOP` where LEFT gives `LÓP`. Every accented paternal surname in the list diverges the same way, so grade against whichever rule the instructor intends and say which.
- The codes table in the instruction sheet is not text. It is a picture pasted into the document, and its contents are transcribed above from the picture data. Both halves of it, dates and fractions, use Spanish codes and return Spanish month and day names.
- Nothing in the instruction sheet mentions the fraction block at B67:G71 on the `Text` sheet. Its six column headings match the six fraction codes in the picture, and the four row labels are Pi, e, Root of 2 and Root of 3, but the source never states which code belongs to which column, and no cell in the block holds a value.
- Two names carry a trailing space, `angélica ` in C22 and `lucía ` in C26 on the `Text` sheet, and the same two on the `Dates` sheet. Any concatenation that adds its own separator produces a double space on those two rows.
- Several surnames are multi word, `de la fuente`, `de santa ana`, `cabeza de vaca` and `martín del campo` among them. PROPER capitalises every word of those, which is what the `Dates` sheet already holds, so the two sheets agree on the point.
- The two sheets differ only in the case of the names and in the answer column headings. The date of birth, the ID and the order of the students are identical, which is why the `Dates` sheet can be used to check the PROPER column.
- The workbook carries no conditional formatting, no data validation, no sheet protection and no charts. The only formatting worth recording is the merged banner B1:F1 on the `Text` sheet and the `dd-mmm-yyyy` number format on both date of birth columns.
