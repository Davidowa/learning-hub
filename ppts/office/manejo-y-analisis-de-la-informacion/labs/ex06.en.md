# Exercise 06 · Basic functions and COUNTIF

Two sheets of one workbook, a log of 38 sales orders and a payroll of seven people, each with a block of questions beside it. The student writes twelve summary formulas: six that use the statistical functions sitting under AutoSum, and six that count or add only the rows meeting a condition. It lands in week 4, sessions 7 and 8, the block the syllabus describes as SUM, AVERAGE, MAX, MIN, COUNTA and COUNTBLANK. Hand in the workbook with L3:L8 and L13:L16 filled on `Orders`, the six answers filled on `Employees`, the registration numbers completed, and the employee table formatted.

**Objectives** MO-200 4.2.1, MO-200 4.2.2, MO-200 2.1.2, MO-200 3.1.1, MO-200 3.1.2, MO-201 3.1.1

## What to do

**On `Orders`.**

1. Fill L3:L8 against the six labels in K3:K8, one function per row, using the Function Library rather than typing. `Maximum quantity` is MAX over F3:F40, `Minimum quantity` is MIN over the same range, `Average full price` is AVERAGE over G3:G40, `No date` is COUNTBLANK over D3:D40, `With full date` is COUNTA over the same range, and `Total shipment costs` is SUM over H3:H40. Routes MO-200 4.2.1 and 4.2.2.
2. Fill L13:L16 against the four labels in K13:K16 with COUNTIF, one criterion each. Route MO-201 3.1.1, part A.
   - L13, orders with quantity above 20: `=COUNTIF(F3:F40,">20")`
   - L14, orders with price of 20 or less: `=COUNTIF(E3:E40,"<=20")`
   - L15, orders from Compañía D: `=COUNTIF(B3:B40,"Compañía D")`
   - L16, orders with quantity between 100 and 300: COUNTIF takes one criterion, so subtract one count from another, `=COUNTIF(F3:F40,">=100")-COUNTIF(F3:F40,">300")`
3. Write every criterion so that it can only come out TRUE or FALSE. These are the operators a COUNTIF criterion accepts, and they go inside the quotation marks:

| Operator | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `=` | equal to |
| `>` | greater than |
| `<` | less than |
| `>=` | greater than or equal to |
| `<=` | less than or equal to |
| `<>` | not equal to |

**On `Employees`.**

4. Complete the registration numbers in B4:B9 from the seed 25 in B3. Use Fill and the Series dialog, not the fill handle. Route MO-200 2.1.2.
5. Give B2:H9 a format. The original leaves the choice open, so pick one route and say which you used: convert the range to an Excel table and apply a named table style (MO-200 3.1.1 then 3.1.2), or leave it as a range and apply cell styles to the header and the body (MO-200 2.2.7). The table route is the one the certification asks about more often.
6. Answer the six questions in column B, one formula each, results in F13, F15, F17, F19, F21 and F23.
   - Employees with no children: `=COUNTBLANK(H3:H9)`
   - Employees with children: `=COUNT(H3:H9)`
   - Employees in the Marketing area: `=COUNTIF(D3:D9,"MKT")`
   - Employees earning above 1000: `=COUNTIF(F3:F9,">1000")`
   - Total number of employees: `=COUNTA(C3:C9)`
   - Total amount of salaries: `=SUM(F3:F9)`

## Exam routes used here

**MO-200 4.2.1, calculations with AVERAGE, MAX, MIN and SUM.** Click the result cell. Go to the **Formulas** tab, **Function Library** group, and click the arrow under **AutoSum**. Pick **Sum**, **Average**, **Max**, **Min** or **Count Numbers**; the plain button face applies Sum. Excel proposes a range with a moving dashed border, so if the guess is wrong drag over the correct range while the function is still open. Press `Enter`. Through the dialog instead: **Formulas** tab, **Function Library** group, **Insert Function**, then in the **Insert Function** dialog open **Or select a category** and pick **Math & Trig** for SUM or **Statistical** for AVERAGE, MAX and MIN, select the function in **Select a function**, click **OK**, click into the **Number1** box of **Function Arguments** and drag the range, read **Formula result =** at the bottom left, and click **OK**. Shortcut: `Alt+=` inserts SUM with a guessed range, `Shift+F3` opens **Insert Function**.

![The Insert Function dialog](../../../img/en/insert-function.png)

*Insert Function, the dialog half of this route, and the way in when the AutoSum list does not carry the function you need. Open the Or select a category list to reach Math & Trig for SUM or Statistical for AVERAGE, MAX and MIN, and read the syntax line under the list before clicking OK.*

**MO-200 4.2.2, counting with COUNT, COUNTA and COUNTBLANK.** Click the result cell. Go to the **Formulas** tab, **Function Library** group, click **More Functions**, point to **Statistical**, and click **COUNT**, **COUNTA** or **COUNTBLANK**. In **Function Arguments**, use the **Value1** box for COUNT and COUNTA and the **Range** box for COUNTBLANK, and drag over the range. Read **Formula result =**, then click **OK**. The three do not overlap the way people assume: on a range holding a number, a text string, a formula returning `""` and one truly empty cell, COUNT returns 1, COUNTA returns 3 and COUNTBLANK returns 2, because both COUNTA and COUNTBLANK claim the cell holding `""`.

**MO-201 3.1.1, part A, one conditional function with no nesting.** Select the cell. Go to the **Formulas** tab, **Function Library** group. COUNTIF is not in a top-level gallery: click **More Functions**, then **Statistical**, then **COUNTIF**. The **Function Arguments** dialog opens with two boxes, **Range** and **Criteria**, in that order. Click into **Range** and drag on the sheet, using the collapse button at the right of the box if the dialog covers the data. Press `Tab`, type the criterion into **Criteria**, and watch the value Excel shows to the right of the box and the **Formula result =** line at the bottom. Click **OK**. SUMIF and SUMIFS sit under **Math & Trig** rather than Statistical, which is worth knowing before week 8.

**MO-200 2.1.2, Auto Fill.** Type the first value in B3. Select B3 together with B4:B9, because the selection defines how far the fill runs. Go to the **Home** tab, **Editing** group, click **Fill**, then **Series...**. In the **Series** dialog set **Series in** to **Columns**, **Type** to **Linear**, and **Step value** to 1. Leave **Trend** clear. Click **OK**. Dragging the fill handle reaches the same numbers, cannot set a Stop value, and leaves an **Auto Fill Options** button at the bottom right of the filled block that the ribbon route does not produce. That button is the visible tell of which route was taken.

**MO-200 3.1.1, create a table from a range, then 3.1.2, apply a table style.** Click any single cell inside B2:H9. Go to the **Insert** tab, **Tables** group, and click **Table**. In the **Create Table** dialog read the box under **Where is the data for your table?** and check that the address covers the header row and the last row of data. Select **My table has headers**. Click **OK**, then confirm the contextual **Table Design** tab appeared. For the style, click any cell in the table, go to **Table Design**, and in the **Table Styles** group click the **More** arrow at the bottom right of the gallery. Point at a style to read its literal name in the tooltip, for example "Green, Table Style Medium 7", then click it. `Ctrl+T` opens the Create Table dialog; the style gallery has no shortcut.

![The Create Table dialog](../../../img/en/create-table.png)

*Create Table, the one box on this route that has anything to check. The shot was taken on another workbook, so the address in it reads `$A$1:$C$4` and not the `$B$2:$H$9` you should be looking at; read whatever Excel proposes against the header row and the last row of the employee table, and leave My table has headers ticked so row 2 stays a header.*

## Notes on the source

- The Spanish instructions name the first block `L3:L6`. The label block is six rows deep, K3 to K8, and the six functions the instructions list match those six labels one for one, so the range is L3:L8.
- The instructions close that block with "see the Formulario Innovatiq file". No such file ships in the exercise folder, and nothing in the block needs it.
- The last COUNTIF, orders with quantity between 100 and 300, cannot be answered by a single COUNTIF, because COUNTIF takes one criterion. It is two COUNTIFs subtracted, as written above, or COUNTIFS, which the syllabus does not reach until week 8.
- The `Orders` sheet keeps its Spanish client and product names. They are data, and the COUNTIF criterion in L15 has to match one of them character for character, accent included.
