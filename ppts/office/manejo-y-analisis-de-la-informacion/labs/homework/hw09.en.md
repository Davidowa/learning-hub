## Argument order, which is the whole trick

`SUMIF` and `AVERAGEIF` take the range you are testing first and the range you are adding last:

```
SUMIF(range, criteria, [sum_range])
AVERAGEIF(range, criteria, [average_range])
```

`COUNTIF` has nothing to add up, so it takes two arguments only:

```
COUNTIF(range, criteria)
```

Their plural cousins in Homework 11 put the range being summarised first instead, `SUMIFS(sum_range, criteria_range1, criteria1, ...)`. That reversal is the single most common mistake in this block of the syllabus, and it is why the singular versions come first.

## Exam routes used here

**Expert 3.1.1, one function, no nesting.** Select the cell that will hold the formula. Go to the **Formulas** tab, **Function Library** group. The conditional-aggregation family is not in a top-level gallery: click **More Functions**, then **Statistical** for `COUNTIF` and `AVERAGEIF`, and use **Math & Trig** for `SUMIF`. Click the function name. The **Function Arguments** dialog opens, titled with the function name. Click into the first argument box; its label is the argument name and the dialog shows a description of that argument underneath as you move between boxes. Type the reference, or click the collapse button at the right of the box, drag the range on the sheet, and click the button again to expand the dialog. Press `Tab` to move to the next box. Excel evaluates each argument live and shows the value to the right of the box, and **Formula result =** at the bottom shows what the cell will hold. Click **OK** and the dialog writes the finished formula into the cell.

For a criterion that is not a plain equals, type the operator inside the box with the value: `>=100`, `<>NP`, `<01/01/2023`. For a criterion that lives in a cell, click the cell instead of typing. For a criterion that combines a cell with an operator, type the operator in quotation marks and join it with an ampersand: `">="&G4`.

**4.2.1 Perform calculations by using the AVERAGE(), MAX(), MIN(), and SUM() functions.** Click the result cell, directly under the column you are summarising. **Formulas** tab, **Function Library** group, arrow under **AutoSum**, then **Sum**, **Average**, **Max**, **Min** or **Count Numbers**; the plain button face applies **Sum**. Excel proposes a range with a moving dashed border and you can drag over the right one while the function is still open. Press `Enter`. `Alt+=` inserts SUM with a guessed range.

**2.2.5 Apply number formats.** Select the numbers only. **Home** tab, **Number** group, open the **Number Format** list and pick **Currency** or **Percentage**, then set the decimals with **Increase Decimal** and **Decrease Decimal**.

## A defect in the source, and what was done about it

In the original `Homework Excel 9.xlsx`, the five item names in the Ej1 summary are padded with spaces. G4 holds `" Computer "` with a space at each end, G5 holds `" Laptop  "` with one space in front and two behind, and so on down the block, while column B of the data holds `"Computer"` clean and `"Laptop "` with a single trailing space. `SUMIF` compares text exactly, spaces included, so every one of the five criteria matches nothing and the summary comes out as five zeros with no error message to explain why.

The CSV export in `../data/` trims leading and trailing spaces from every text value, so the version of the data linked from this page does not carry the fault. Anyone working from the original workbook has three ways out, in descending order of how much they teach:

1. Clean the labels. Retype G4:G8 without the padding, or overwrite them with `=TRIM()` and paste the results back as values.
2. Wrap the criterion: `=SUMIF($B$4:$B$23,TRIM(G4)&"*",$C$4:$C$23)`. The `TRIM` removes the padding on the label and the `*` absorbs the stray trailing space in the data.
3. Type the item names into the criteria by hand, which works and teaches nothing, because the point of pointing a criterion at a cell is that the summary follows when the label changes.

The names in Ej3 column A carry four leading spaces each, used as indentation. That one is harmless, because nothing compares against them, and the CSV trims it as well.
