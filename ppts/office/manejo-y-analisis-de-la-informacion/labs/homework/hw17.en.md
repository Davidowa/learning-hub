# Homework 17 · Charts that answer a question

The take-home twin of Exercise 17. Two datasets and eight charts, and the point of every one is the type chosen rather than the colours applied. The tasks are the instructor's own, from the Instructions sheet.

**Objectives** MO-200 5.1.1 · 5.2.3 · 5.3.1

## The data

**accounts**, 6 rows and 4 columns. [../data/hw17-accounts.csv](../data/hw17-accounts.csv)

**population**, 33 rows and 10 columns. [../data/hw17-population.csv](../data/hw17-population.csv)

**portal log**, 22 rows and 8 columns. [../data/hw17-portal-log.csv](../data/hw17-portal-log.csv)
## What to do

The Instructions sheet carries two exercises. Both are headed EXERCISE 1 in the workbook and both number their charts from one, so read the second heading as exercise 2.

**Exercise 1, the web portal.** You are building a portal on the web, and you want to measure the growth in registered accounts and in the courtesy accounts granted to journalists and to other companies. Use the data provided to make each of the following charts.

1. A 3D column chart of active accounts by category. Change the colour of each of the bars.
2. A line chart with markers showing the accesses to the portal by the internal people over time. Change the title to `Internal access per day`.
3. A stacked bar chart with a 3D effect showing the press people and the guests who entered per day. Change the colour of each element, and adjust the layout so that every field name appears.
4. A chart of lines and bars with two series, one for the times of sending and one for the mails sent across the month.
5. A pie chart with a 3D effect comparing the accesses of company A and company B across the month. Make sure both are properly labelled and that the percentage held by each company is shown.

**Exercise 2, state population.** Population figures for the states across several decades beginning in 1950. Produce the following charts.

1. A column chart with a 3D effect comparing Jalisco, Veracruz, Nuevo León, Distrito Federal and Estado de México over the years.
2. A line chart showing the information for all of the states over the years.
3. A map chart of the results for the year 2000.

- The fifth chart of exercise 1 is written as an area chart with pie chart in brackets, and those are two different types, so which one was meant is not certain. The percentage labels asked for in the same sentence point at the pie.
- The map chart is the one to check your Excel version on before promising it to a group.
