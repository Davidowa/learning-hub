## Exam routes used here

**2.2.1 Merge and unmerge cells.** Select the range to merge; only the top left cell keeps its value. Go to the **Home** tab, **Alignment** group, and click the **arrow on Merge & Center**, not the button face. Pick **Merge & Center** to merge the whole selection and centre it, **Merge Across** to merge each row of the selection separately, or **Merge Cells** to merge without touching the alignment. Read the warning about discarded values before clicking **OK**. To unmerge, same arrow, **Unmerge Cells**. The dialog route, which is the one to use when the task also asks for other formatting: select the range, press **Ctrl+1**, go to the **Alignment** tab, select the **Merge cells** check box in the **Text control** section, set Horizontal and Vertical in the same visit, click **OK**.

**2.2.2 Modify cell alignment, orientation, and indentation.** Select the range. Press **Ctrl+1**, or **Home** tab, **Alignment** group, dialog box launcher. **Format Cells** opens on the **Alignment** tab. In **Text alignment**, open **Horizontal** and pick from **General**, **Left (Indent)**, **Center**, **Right (Indent)**, **Fill**, **Justify**, **Center Across Selection**, **Distributed (Indent)**. Open **Vertical** for **Top**, **Center**, **Bottom**, **Justify**, **Distributed**. The **Indent** spin box only comes alive once Horizontal is one of the Indent entries, so set Horizontal first. In the **Orientation** section on the right, drag the red diamond on the half dial or type the angle into the **Degrees** box, range -90 to 90; for letters stacked down the cell, click the tall narrow box that reads **Text** vertically. Set **Wrap text**, **Shrink to fit** and **Merge cells** in the same visit. Click **OK** once.

**2.2.4 Wrap text within cells.** Select the range. Press **Ctrl+1**, click the **Alignment** tab, and in the **Text control** section select the **Wrap text** check box. While the dialog is open set anything the task pairs with it, normally **Vertical** to **Top** so the wrapped lines start at the top of the cell. Click **OK**. **Shrink to fit** and **Wrap text** are mutually exclusive; selecting one clears the other, and a task that says "make it fit without changing the row height" wants Shrink to fit.

**2.2.5 Apply number formats.** Select the numbers only, not the header. Go to the **Home** tab, **Number** group. Open the **Number Format** list at the top of the group, which reads **General** by default, and pick **Currency**, **Percentage** or whichever the task names. Adjust with the buttons underneath: **Accounting Number Format** with its arrow for the currency symbol, **Percent Style**, **Comma Style**, **Increase Decimal**, **Decrease Decimal**. When the task names a number of decimal places, click Increase Decimal or Decrease Decimal until the count is right rather than retyping the numbers. Currency and Accounting are not the same and the exam knows it: Currency puts the symbol tight against the digits, Accounting lines the symbols up on the left edge, shows zero as a dash and puts negatives in parentheses.

**2.2.6 Apply cell formats from the Format Cells dialog box.** This is the objective the whole homework is built around. Select the range and press **Ctrl+1**, or click the dialog box launcher of the **Font**, **Alignment** or **Number** group on the **Home** tab; all three open the same dialog on a different tab. The dialog has six tabs: **Number**, **Alignment**, **Font**, **Border**, **Fill**, **Protection**. On the **Font** tab set **Font**, **Font style**, **Size**, the **Underline** list and the **Color** list, with **More Colors...** for anything off the palette. Do not click OK. Click the **Fill** tab and pick a swatch under **Background Color**. Still without closing, click the **Border** tab, and here the order matters more than anywhere else in Excel: choose the **Style** from the Line box and the **Color** from the Color list first, then click a **Presets** button (**None**, **Outline**, **Inside**) or click the individual edges in the **Border** preview box. A border drawn before its style is set comes out in the previous style, and setting the style afterwards does nothing to it. Click the **Number** tab for **Category** and its options, the **Alignment** tab for Horizontal, Vertical, Indent and Orientation, and only then click **OK** once. Everything lands as a single operation.

The proof of which route was taken is one press of **Ctrl+Z**. From the dialog, the font colour and the fill disappear together. From the ribbon it takes two presses.

**1.3.2 Adjust row height and column width.** Select the rows or columns by clicking their headings; drag across headings, or `Ctrl`-click for a non-adjacent set. Go to the **Home** tab, **Cells** group, and click **Format**. For rows click **Row Height...** and type the value in points. For columns click **Column Width...** and type the value in characters of the standard font, which is why 20 is a wide column and 20 is a short row. To size to content instead, click **Format** and click **AutoFit Row Height** or **AutoFit Column Width**.
