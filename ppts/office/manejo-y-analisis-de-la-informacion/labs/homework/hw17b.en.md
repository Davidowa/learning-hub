# Homework 17b · Climate data, seven chart families

The second half of homework 17, and the better one. A hundred and fifty-six rows of state climate data taken through seven chart types, including the two that make people argue. The tasks are the instructor's own, from the Instructions sheet.

**Objectives** MO-200 5.1.1 · 5.2.3 · MO-201 4.1.1

## The data

**max temperature**, 33 rows and 13 columns. [../data/hw17b-max-temperature.csv](../data/hw17b-max-temperature.csv)

**mean temperature**, 33 rows and 13 columns. [../data/hw17b-mean-temperature.csv](../data/hw17b-mean-temperature.csv)

**min temperature**, 33 rows and 13 columns. [../data/hw17b-min-temperature.csv](../data/hw17b-min-temperature.csv)

**precipitation**, 33 rows and 13 columns. [../data/hw17b-precipitation.csv](../data/hw17b-precipitation.csv)
## What to do

- Columns. National maximum temperature over time, then a clustered column of maximum and minimum for each month.
- Bars. Average annual temperature by state, sorted high to low, then filtered to the ten hottest.
- Lines. Turn the clustered column of maxima and minima into a line chart and say what improved.
- Combo. National average temperature against average precipitation over time, which is the chart that needs a second axis.
- Pie, then hierarchy. Cumulative precipitation by state as a pie, then as a treemap, then compare them. That comparison is the exercise.
- Map. Accumulated precipitation by state.
- The sheet is named Cimate DAta in the workbook, spelled exactly that way.

Before handing it in, confirm the row count of every result against the source, and make sure any sort or filter you applied is either removed or deliberate. Hand in the workbook as `Homework 17b Excel_Your name.xlsx`.
