# Homework 7 · The IF function

Three small tables, three IF formulas, and no nesting anywhere. The point is the shape of the function rather than its cleverness: a comparison, a value when it is true, a value when it is false, and a decision about what the false branch should say. It lands in week 5, session 10, the session that opens IF. Hand in the finished workbook as `Homework 7 Excel_Your name.xlsx`.

## What to do

1. In column E of Exercise 1, show `Give Notice` for every member with two or more outstanding monthly payments. Route 4.2.3. Decide what the other members should show and be consistent: an empty string, `""`, leaves the cell looking blank while keeping a formula in it.
2. In column D of Exercise 2, show `YES` for every family with three children or more. Route 4.2.3.
3. In column D of Exercise 3, calculate a ten per cent discount, but only on tickets paid in cash. Every other payment method gets zero. Route 4.2.3, with the test written against `CASH` rather than against `CARD`.
4. In column E of Exercise 3, calculate the total payable as the subtotal minus the discount. This one is arithmetic, not a function.
5. Format the money columns of Exercise 3 as currency with two decimal places. Route 2.2.5.
6. Save as `Homework 7 Excel_Your name.xlsx` and upload it to Moodle.

## Exam routes used here

**4.2.3 Perform conditional operations by using the IF() function.** Click the result cell. Go to the **Formulas** tab, **Function Library** group, click **Logical**, and click **IF**. In the **Function Arguments** dialog, click in the **Logical_test** box and build the comparison, for example click cell C5 and type `>=2`. Click in the **Value_if_true** box and type the text without quotation marks; the dialog adds them for you and shows the finished value to the right of the box. Click in the **Value_if_false** box and do the same. Leaving Value_if_false empty returns `FALSE`, which is almost never what the task wants; for a cell that should look blank, type `""` there. Read **Formula result =** at the bottom of the dialog, click **OK**, lock any reference that must not move by putting the insertion point on it in the formula bar and pressing `F4`, then fill down and spot check one row on each side of the boundary.

![Function Arguments, open on IF](../../../../img/en/function-arguments-if.png)

*The value each box will produce is printed to its right. That is the quickest way to catch a Value_if_false that was left empty and is about to write FALSE down the whole column.*

The short route is typing `=IF(C5>=2,"Give Notice","")` straight into the cell. It gives the same answer and shows nothing about which route was taken, so learn it and know that an item worded "use the IF function" from the Function Library earns nothing for it. `Shift+F3` opens **Insert Function** on an empty cell and reopens **Function Arguments** when the insertion point is already inside a function.

**2.2.5 Apply number formats.** Select the numbers only, not the header. **Home** tab, **Number** group. Open the **Number Format** list at the top of the group and pick **Currency**, then adjust the decimals with **Increase Decimal** and **Decrease Decimal**. Currency and Accounting are not the same: Currency puts the symbol tight against the digits, Accounting lines the symbols up on the left edge, shows zero as a dash and puts negatives in parentheses.

**Expert 3.1.1, the IF half.** The graded route never types the function blind. **Formulas** tab, **Function Library** group, **Logical**, **IF**. Click into the first argument box; its label is the argument name and the dialog shows a description of that argument as you move between the boxes. Type the reference, or click the collapse button at the right of the box and drag the range on the sheet. Press `Tab` to move on. Excel evaluates each argument live and shows the value to the right of the box, and the **Formula result =** line at the bottom shows what the cell will hold. Click **OK** and the dialog writes the finished formula into the cell.

## Notes on the source

`Homework Excel 7.xlsx` puts the three problems on one sheet, `Hoja1`, with each instruction typed into the cell above its table rather than gathered on an instruction sheet. The member names are placeholders, `Member 1` through `Member 8`. Exercise 3 is the only one of the three where the false branch carries a number rather than text, and it is also the only one with three possible values in the column being tested, which is what makes it the interesting one.
