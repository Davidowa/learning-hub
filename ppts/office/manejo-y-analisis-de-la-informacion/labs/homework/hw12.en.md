# Homework 12 · Three years of unit sales, ready to summarise

The take-home twin of Exercise 12. A thousand product rows with three years of sales side by side, which is the shape a PivotTable was built for.

**Objectives** MO-201 4.2.1 · MO-201 2.2.4

## The data

**car sales**, 98 rows and 9 columns. [../data/hw12-car-sales.csv](../data/hw12-car-sales.csv)

**tax table**, 5 rows and 2 columns. [../data/hw12-tax-table.csv](../data/hw12-tax-table.csv)

The Data sheet opens with `SALES IN UNITS` in A1 and `Segment:  Luxury Cars` in A2, a banner the data does not quite bear out: column C holds both `Luxury` and `High`, and column F holds both `National` and `Imported`, which is what makes all four rows of the tax table reachable. The table header is row 8 and the models run from row 9 to row 105. Columns A to I hold theme, type, segment, brand, model, origin and the units sold in 2018, 2019 and 2020. Row 107 closes the sheet with `General total` in E107 and `=SUM(G9:G105)` filled across G, H and I, which is the only formula the workbook ships. Everything else is yours to build.

The answer columns are already headed and empty, and their headers are the shortest description of the assignment there is. K, L and M sit under a `Sales Participation` banner in K6 and are headed `2018`, `2019` and `2020`. N is `Change in participation 2020 vs 2019`. P sits under `Variation in Sales` in P6 and is headed `2020 vs 2019`. Q is `Type of variation`, R is `Trend`, T is `% Tax`, V is `Classification`. The tax table itself lives on this sheet as well, in `X8:Y12`, headed `Car Type` and `% Tax`.

The sheet carries three conditional formatting rules, and all three sit on the single cell `Q106`. Each one is a **Cell Value** rule, **equal to** a piece of text, and each sets a fill and nothing else:

| Priority | Condition | Fill |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | equal to `"Crecimiento en Ventas"` | `FFCDF3CD`, a light green |
| 2 | equal to `"Decremento moderado en Ventas"` | `FFF7CAAC`, a light orange |
| 3 | equal to `"Decremento grave en Ventas"` | `FFFFB3B3`, a light red |

Two things about them. `Q106` is the empty row between the last model and the total row, so as shipped the rules colour nothing; to put them to work, open **Manage Rules...** and widen **Applies to** to `Q9:Q105`. And the three strings are Spanish in a workbook whose instructions, headers and data are English. An equal-to rule matches the text exactly, so the wording in column Q and the wording in the rule have to be the same string, character for character, or the fill never appears.

## What to do

Eight tasks, all of them filled down the whole table, rows 9 to 105.

1. **Sales share**, columns K, L and M. For each of the three years, the model's share of that year's total sales, as a percentage. The denominator is the year's own total, so lock it before filling down.
2. **Change in share, 2020 against 2019**, column N. Not a number: the word `Increase` or the word `Decrease`, depending on which way the share moved.
3. **Change in sales, 2020 against 2019**, column P. The percentage change in units sold between the two years.
4. **Type of variation**, column Q. A positive variation is `Increase in Sales`. A fall of up to 20% is `Moderate decrease in Sales`. A fall beyond that is `Severe decrease in sales`, in the source's own lower case. Where the variation reads `N/A`, the type of variation reads `N/A` as well.
5. **Trend**, column R. A sparkline per row over the three units-sold figures in G, H and I.
6. **% Tax**, column T. Read the rate off the table in `X8:Y12`, which pairs origin with segment. This is the one task that names its function: `IFS`, `SI.CONJUNTO` in the Spanish interface.
7. **Classification**, column V. `Relevant` if the average of the three sales shares is above 10%, or the 2020 variation is above 8%. Otherwise `Not Relevant`.
8. **Freeze panes** so that the `Origin` column and the table header stay visible while you scroll. Origin is column F and the header is row 8, so the cell to click before freezing is `G9`.

Three places where the original wording is loose, left as found rather than resolved. Task 4 writes the last band as "greater than -20%", which read as arithmetic says the opposite of what it means; the sense is a fall of more than twenty per cent. Task 4 also asks for `N/A` without saying where it comes from: eight models have no 2019 figure, blank in column H for six of them and a zero for two, and a percentage change against nothing is what the `N/A` is there to absorb. Task 7 says `Relevant` when either condition holds and then says it is `Not Relevant` if any condition fails, which are two different rules, an OR and an AND, and the source does not choose between them.
