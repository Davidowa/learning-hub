## The data

**farmers market**, 85 rows and 5 columns. [../data/hw24-farmers-market.csv](../data/hw24-farmers-market.csv)
## What to do

Using the farmers market sales information, generate the following filters, starting in cell `H5`. Each one is an advanced filter with its own criteria, and the source says only that the work starts at `H5`, without saying whether that cell holds the first criteria range or the first output range. Leave room for both and keep the six sets clear of one another.

- Only the observations whose sales are greater than `$100,000`.
- Products with the location Center and the season Autumn. The data spells the season `Autum`, and the criterion has to match the data rather than the dictionary.
- Meat products in both Spring and Summer. Two values on one field means two criteria rows, one under the other, not two columns side by side.
- Products with higher than average sales. This is the one that needs a computed criterion: a heading that is blank, or at least different from every heading in the data, with a formula under it comparing the first data row against the whole sales column, `=E6>AVERAGE($E$6:$E$89)`.
- Products whose code is greater than 10 and less than 40. The codes run 10 to 60 in tens, so this filter returns codes 20 and 30 and nothing else.
- Spring Vegetable products with sales of less than 50,000, showing only the product and the sales. The output range for this one carries just those two headings, which is what tells the filter to drop the other three columns.

- The sheet starts on row 5 with code, location, product, season and sales, so the list range is `A5:E89`, header row included.
- Five product families across three seasons and several locations, so every total can be checked two ways, down the column and across the season.
- Autumn is spelled Autum in the data, in every row. Leave it. It is what the file says, and a filter written against the correct spelling returns nothing, which is the lesson.
