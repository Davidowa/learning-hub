# Homework 5 · Operator order, absolute and mixed references

Four short problems on one sheet, each one of them broken by a formula that was filled without locking something. The second problem is the one that matters most, because it is the only place in the whole homework set that forces a mixed reference: a grid filled in two directions at once, where locking both the row and the column gives the wrong answer just as surely as locking neither. It lands in week 3, session 6, the session that opens the Formulas tab. Hand in the finished workbook as `Homework 5 Excel_Your name.xlsx`.

**Objectives** MO-200 2.2.5, 4.1.1, 4.2.1

## What to do

1. **Exercise 1.** In C5 write a formula that multiplies A5 by B5, lock the multiplier so it does not move, and fill it down to C16. Route 4.1.1. One press of `F4` on the reference to B5 gives `$B$5`, which is what this task wants.
2. **Exercise 2.** In B23 write one formula that multiplies the number at the left of the row by the number at the top of the column, and fill it right to E23 and down to E26 without editing it again. Route 4.1.1, and this is the mixed-reference case: lock the column of the left-hand value and the row of the top value, so the formula reads `=$A23*B$22`. Fully absolute breaks it, fully relative breaks it, and only the mixed form survives being dragged in two directions.
3. **Exercise 3, part 1.** In F34 calculate the annual total for the first seller and fill it down to F40. Route 4.2.1.
4. **Exercise 3, part 2.** On row 41 calculate the total for each period, B41 to F41. Route 4.2.1.
5. **Exercise 3, part 3.** In G34 calculate the share of the annual grand total that each seller represents, and fill it down. The grand total in F41 has to be locked, so the formula reads `=F34/$F$41`. Route 4.1.1. Format the column as a percentage with two decimal places. Route 2.2.5.
6. **Exercise 4, part 1.** In B55 convert 3,000 pesos to dollars and in C55 to euros, then fill both down to row 57. The two rates in B49 and B50 must be locked. Route 4.1.1.
7. **Exercise 4, part 2.** In B63 convert dollars back to pesos and in D63 convert euros back to pesos, then fill both down to row 65. Same locking, opposite arithmetic.
8. Save as `Homework 5 Excel_Your name.xlsx` and upload it to Moodle.

## Exam routes used here

**4.1.1 Insert relative, absolute, and mixed references.** Click the cell that will hold the formula and type `=`. Click the cell you want to refer to, or type its address; it arrives relative, as `B5`. Leave the insertion point touching that reference and press `F4`. Each press cycles one step: `B5`, `$B$5`, `B$5`, `$B5`, and back to `B5`. Stop on the form the task needs. `$B$5` locks column and row. `B$5` locks the row only, so the reference slides sideways when filled right and stays on row 5 when filled down. `$B5` locks the column only. Press `Enter`, then fill in both directions with the fill handle or with `Ctrl+R` and `Ctrl+D`. Click a cell in the far corner of the filled block and read the formula bar to confirm the locked part did not move.

That last step is the whole of Exercise 2. Fill `=$A23*B$22` across and down, then click E26 and read the bar: it must say `=$A26*E$22`. The column letter of the first reference and the row number of the second are the two things that did not move.

**4.2.1 Perform calculations by using the AVERAGE(), MAX(), MIN(), and SUM() functions.** Click the result cell, and put it directly under the column or to the right of the row you are summarising so Excel guesses well. Go to the **Formulas** tab, **Function Library** group, and click the arrow under **AutoSum**. Pick **Sum**, **Average**, **Max**, **Min** or **Count Numbers**; the plain button face applies **Sum**. Excel writes the function and proposes a range with a moving dashed border. If the proposal is wrong, drag over the correct range now, while the function is still open, and Excel replaces the argument. Press `Enter`.

Through the dialog instead: click the result cell, **Formulas** tab, **Function Library** group, **Insert Function**. In the **Insert Function** dialog open the **Or select a category** list and pick **Math & Trig** for SUM or **Statistical** for AVERAGE, MAX and MIN, select the function in the **Select a function** list, and click **OK**. In the **Function Arguments** dialog click in the **Number1** box and drag over the range, using the collapse button at the right of the box if the dialog covers the data. Read **Formula result =** at the bottom left before committing, then click **OK**. `Alt+=` inserts SUM with a guessed range; `Shift+F3` opens **Insert Function**.

![The Insert Function dialog](../../../../img/en/insert-function.png)

*The Or select a category list opens on Most Recently Used, and it is where Math and Trig hides SUM while Statistical hides AVERAGE, MAX and MIN. The line under the Select a function list shows the syntax of whichever function is highlighted.*

**2.2.5 Apply number formats.** Select the numbers only, not the header. **Home** tab, **Number** group. Open the **Number Format** list at the top of the group and pick **Percentage**, then click **Increase Decimal** until two decimals show. The **Percent Style** button in the same group applies zero decimals, so the two-decimal part still has to be clicked in.

## Checks

- Click C16. The formula bar reads `=A16*$B$5`. The `$B$5` is unchanged from C5.
- Click E26. The formula bar reads `=$A26*E$22`. If it reads `=$A$23*$B$22` the formula was locked everywhere and only B23 is right. If it reads `=D26*E25` nothing was locked and the whole grid is wrong.
- The grid reads 200, 250, 260, 300 across its first row and 800, 1000, 1040, 1200 across its last.
- F41 is the sum of F34:F40 and also the sum of B41:E41. The two agree, which is the arithmetic check that both totals were built correctly.
- G34:G40 shows percentages with two decimals and adds up to 100.00%.
- Click G40. The formula bar reads `=F40/$F$41`, not `=F40/F47`.
- 3,000 pesos comes out as 174.93 dollars and 158.48 euros at the rates given.
- 1,700 dollars comes out as 29,155.00 pesos, and 2,000 euros as 37,860.00.

## Notes on the source

`Homework Excel 5.xlsx` keeps its four problems on a single sheet named `Ejercicios`, with the instructions typed into cells rather than a text box. Two of them are worded loosely. Exercise 2 says "Multiply column A by row 20" when the numbers it means are on row 22; the intent is unambiguous from the layout. Exercise 1 puts the multiplier in B5, on the same row as the first value, which makes it easy to select A5 and B5 together by accident; the multiplier is a single cell, not part of the list. The empty sheets `Hoja2` and `Hoja3` are left over from the template and can be ignored or deleted.
