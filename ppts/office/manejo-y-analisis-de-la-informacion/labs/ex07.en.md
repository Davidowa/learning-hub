# Exercise 07 · The IF function

Three small sheets, each one a different shape of the same decision. A grade sheet that turns an average into Pass or Fail, a travel agency that pays two commission rates depending on the sector, and a shop that adds a card fee or takes a cash discount off the same list price. It lands in week 5, sessions 9 and 10, where the syllabus opens the IF function, and it comes back in week 6, session 11, when IF is taught in depth. Hand in the workbook with columns E and F filled on `Exercise1`, column D and the answer block filled on `Exercise2`, and column D filled on `Exercise3`.

**Objectives** MO-200 4.2.3, MO-200 4.1.1, MO-200 4.1.2, MO-200 2.3.1, MO-200 2.1.1, MO-200 4.2.1, MO-200 4.2.2, MO-201 3.1.1, MO-210 2.1.5

## What to do

**On `Exercise1`.**

1. Generate the grades yourself. In B2:D8 write `=RANDBETWEEN(5,10)`, which returns a whole number between the two limits, inclusive. Every recalculation of the sheet gives a new set, which is the point of task 2.
2. Freeze them. Select B2:D8, copy, and paste back over the same range as values, so the grades stop moving. Route MO-200 2.1.1, the **Paste Special** dialog with **Values** selected, not the small clipboard icon in the gallery.
3. In E2:E8 calculate each student's average with AVERAGE over B:D of that row. Route MO-200 4.2.1.
4. In F2:F8 write `Pass` when the average is above 5.9 and `Fail` when it is below 6, with one IF. Route MO-200 4.2.3. Either direction of the test works, `=IF(E2>5.9,"Pass","Fail")` or `=IF(E2<=5.9,"Fail","Pass")`, which is the form the shipped workbook uses. Build it in the **Function Arguments** dialog and let the dialog add the quotation marks.

**On `Exercise2`.**

5. Name the two rates before writing anything. Select B7 and define a name for the international rate, select B8 and define a name for the national one. Route MO-200 2.3.1. The workbook ships with these two names already defined as `INT` and `NAT`; see the notes at the end for why `INT` is a poor choice and what to use instead.
6. In D11:D18 calculate the commission as total sales multiplied by the rate that matches the sector, with the rate coming from the name and not from a typed number. Route MO-200 4.1.2 for the name, MO-200 4.2.3 for the decision. The shape is `=C11*IF(B11="INT",Int_rate,Nat_rate)`.
7. In C19 and D19 total the sales and the commissions with SUM.
8. Fill the answer block D21:D26 against the six questions in A21:A26, one function each. Number of employees is COUNT over D11:D18, number in the international sector is `=COUNTIF(B11:B18,"INT")`, number in the national sector is `=COUNTIF(B11:B18,"NAC")`, average sales is AVERAGE over C11:C18, and the highest and lowest commissions are MAX and MIN over D11:D18. Routes MO-200 4.2.1, MO-200 4.2.2 and MO-201 3.1.1.

**On `Exercise3`.**

9. In D8:D17 calculate the total sale price. When the payment method is `Credit Card`, add the fee in D4 to the list price. When it is `Cash`, take the discount in D5 off it. One IF does both: `=IF(C8="Credit Card",B8*(1+$D$4),B8*(1-$D$5))`.
10. Lock the two references to the constants before filling down. Put the insertion point on D4 in the formula bar and press `F4`, then do the same for D5. Route MO-200 4.1.1. Nothing on this sheet is named, so absolute references are the only way to hold the rates still.

## Exam routes used here

**MO-200 4.2.3, conditional operations with IF.** Click the result cell. Go to the **Formulas** tab, **Function Library** group, click **Logical**, and click **IF**. In the **Function Arguments** dialog click into the **Logical_test** box and build the comparison, for example click E2 and type `>5.9`. Click into **Value_if_true** and type the text without quotation marks; the dialog adds them and shows the finished value to the right of the box. Do the same in **Value_if_false**, and do not leave it empty, because an empty box returns FALSE and that is almost never what the task wants. Read **Formula result =** at the bottom, then click **OK**. Lock any reference that must not move before filling down: put the insertion point on it in the formula bar and press `F4`. Fill down and spot check one row on each side of the boundary. Shortcut: `Shift+F3` with the insertion point inside an existing IF reopens **Function Arguments** on that formula, which is the fastest way to repair a condition.

**MO-200 2.3.1, define a named range.** Select the cell holding the rate. Go to the **Formulas** tab, **Defined Names** group, and click **Define Name**. In the **New Name** dialog type the name in the **Name** box. It must start with a letter, an underscore or a backslash, it cannot contain spaces, and it cannot be a cell address. Open the **Scope** list and pick **Workbook** so the name works from any sheet. Check that **Refers to** holds an absolute reference such as `=Exercise2!$B$7`, using the collapse arrow at the right of the box to reselect if it does not. Click **OK**. The Name Box at the left of the formula bar reaches the same result, but it always scopes to Workbook, offers no comment, and never shows the reference it built, so there is no chance to catch a wrong range. Shortcut: `Ctrl+F3` opens **Name Manager**.

**MO-200 4.1.2, reference a named range in a formula.** Inside the formula, start typing the first letters of the name. Formula AutoComplete lists it with a tag icon; press `Tab` to insert it. Or press `F3` to open the **Paste Name** dialog, select the name and click **OK**. Dragging over the cells instead gives `$B$7` and the same arithmetic, and when the task says to use the defined name the address is the wrong answer.

**MO-200 2.1.1, paste as values.** Select B2:D8. Go to the **Home** tab, **Clipboard** group, and click **Copy**, not Cut, because cut disables most of the Paste Special options. Select the destination, here the same top left cell B2. Go back to the **Home** tab, **Clipboard** group, and click the **arrow under the Paste button**, not the button face. At the bottom of the gallery click **Paste Special...**. In the **Paste Special** dialog, **Paste** section, select **Values**. Click **OK**. Press **Esc** to clear the marching border. Shortcut: `Ctrl+Alt+V` opens the dialog directly, and `V` then `Enter` selects Values and commits. To check it, click a pasted cell and read the formula bar: it must hold a number, not `=RANDBETWEEN(5,10)`.

**MO-200 4.2.1 and 4.2.2, the answer block.** AVERAGE, MAX and MIN come off the arrow under **AutoSum** on the **Formulas** tab, **Function Library** group, or from **Insert Function** with **Statistical** selected in **Or select a category**. COUNT is on the same AutoSum list under the name **Count Numbers**, and also under **More Functions**, **Statistical**, **COUNT**.

**MO-201 3.1.1, part A, COUNTIF.** Select the cell. **Formulas** tab, **Function Library** group, **More Functions**, **Statistical**, **COUNTIF**. Fill **Range** and then **Criteria** in the **Function Arguments** dialog, reading the live value beside each box. Click **OK**.

**MO-210 2.1.5, RANDBETWEEN.** This one belongs to the Microsoft 365 exam, not to MO-200, so `procedures.en.md` lists the function in its glossary and carries no click path for it. Use the **Insert Function** route from 4.2.1: **Formulas** tab, **Function Library** group, **Insert Function**, type `RANDBETWEEN` in **Search for a function**, click **Go**, select it, click **OK**, then fill the **Bottom** and **Top** boxes with 5 and 10. The gallery position of the function is not confirmed here and should be checked on the lab build before it is taught as a click path.

## Notes on the source

- Two of the three sheets ship solved. `Exercise1` already holds the AVERAGE and IF formulas in E2:F8, and `Exercise2` already holds the commission column and the whole answer block. Clear E2:F8 and D11:D26 before handing the file to students, or work from the CSV files above, which contain only the input columns.
- `Exercise1` also ships with fixed grades rather than an empty grid, which quietly removes task 1. Overwriting B2:D8 with RANDBETWEEN is the first thing to do.
- The rate table on `Exercise2` labels the national rate `NAT (National)` while every record in column B uses `NAC`. The shipped formula `=C11*IF(B11="INT",INT,NAT)` still returns the right number, because anything that is not INT falls through to the national rate, but the legend and the data disagree and the COUNTIF in D23 has to follow the data, not the legend.
- The two defined names in the shipped workbook are `INT` and `NAT`. `INT` is also the name of a built-in worksheet function. Excel accepts the name and resolves it correctly inside a formula, and it is still a bad habit to teach. Rename them to `Int_rate` and `Nat_rate` in **Name Manager**, which updates every formula that uses them.
- The instructions say Pass above 5.9 and Fail below 6, which leaves the strip between 5.9 and 6 unstated. It never comes up here: three whole-number grades average to a multiple of one third, and no multiple of one third lands in that strip.
- Spelling in the shipped workbook: `Comission` for commission in B6, D10 and the question labels, and `Hightest` for highest in A25. One employee name is `Coria, Guillemo`, which is Guillermo.
