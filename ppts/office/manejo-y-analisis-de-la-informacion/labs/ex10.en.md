# Exercise 10 · AND, OR and wildcards

Three sheets that take the single condition of the previous exercises and stretch it two ways. First sideways, by putting more than one test inside the same decision with AND and OR. Then loosely, by letting a criterion match part of a string instead of all of it. A complete four-input truth table, nine questions about three men and their measurements, and 23 Mexican place names counted nine different ways. It lands in week 7, session 13, which the syllabus describes as operators and wildcards with the AND and OR functions. Hand in the workbook with H, I, J and K filled on `AND-OR`, the nine answers on `IF-AND-OR`, and E2:E10 on `WILDCARDS`.

**Objectives** MO-200 4.2.3, MO-200 1.2.1, MO-200 2.3.1, MO-201 3.1.1

## What to do

**On `AND-OR`.** The three filled columns are the reference. AND returns TRUE only when every argument is TRUE. OR returns TRUE as soon as one of them is. NOT flips whatever it is given. Read E, F and G across a few rows before writing H, I, J and K, then fill the four headed columns to match their headers. Every cell returns TRUE or FALSE and nothing else. Route MO-201 3.1.1, part A.

1. H2:H17, headed `AND 1, 2, 3`: `=AND(A2,B2,C2)`. Criterion 4 is left out, so the column asks whether the first three all hold.
2. I2:I17, headed `OR 1, 2, 3`: `=OR(A2,B2,C2)`.
3. J2:J17, headed `AND 2,4`: `=AND(B2,D2)`.
4. K2:K17, headed `OR 2,4`: `=OR(B2,D2)`.

**On `IF-AND-OR`.** Answer the nine questions with IF, adding AND or OR inside the logical test where the question needs more than one condition. Route MO-200 4.2.3 for the IF and MO-201 3.1.1, part B, for the nesting. Keep all nine answers in one column, one per question row; the shipped workbook scatters them and that is a defect, not a pattern to copy.

5. If José is over 180, show Luis' height, otherwise Carlos' height: `=IF(B2>180,C2,D2)`.
6. If José's hair is Brown show "Brown", otherwise show "Other": `=IF(B8="Brown","Brown","Other")`. Read the note at the end of this file before you decide the question is broken.
7. If José weighs more than Luis, show José's eye colour, otherwise Luis': `=IF(B6>C6,B7,C7)`.
8. If Carlos is older than José, add their ages, otherwise average them: `=IF(D3>B3,D3+B3,AVERAGE(B3,D3))`. Two functions nested in two different argument boxes of the same IF.
9. If José or Luis is blond, show "There are blonds", otherwise "There are no blonds": `=IF(OR(B8="Blond",C8="Blond"),"There are blonds","There are no blonds")`.
10. If José and Luis both have green eyes show "Green", otherwise show Carlos' eye colour: `=IF(AND(B7="Green",C7="Green"),"Green",D7)`.
11. If José, Luis or Carlos weighs more than 100 kilos show "More than 100", otherwise "Less than 100": `=IF(OR(B6>100,C6>100,D6>100),"More than 100","Less than 100")`.
12. If José's height is above 180 and Luis' is above 160, show "Tall", otherwise "Not classified": `=IF(AND(B2>180,C2>160),"Tall","Not classified")`.
13. If José's height is above 180 or Luis' is below 180, show "José is taller", otherwise "José is shorter": `=IF(OR(B2>180,C2<180),"José is taller","José is shorter")`.

**On `WILDCARDS`.**

14. Check the defined name before using it. Open **Name Manager** and read what `Names` refers to. Route MO-200 2.3.1. It ships pointing at A1:A24, header included; repoint it to A2:A24.
15. In E2:E10 count how many names meet each criterion, taking the criterion from column D so the formula reads the pattern from the sheet rather than carrying it inside quotation marks: `=COUNTIF(Names,D2)`. Route MO-201 3.1.1, part A.
16. Do at least one of the nine again through **Find and Replace** instead, so you see the same wildcards working outside a formula. Route MO-200 1.2.1. `Find All` reports how many cells matched, and that number must agree with the COUNTIF beside it.

A criterion has to describe the whole cell, not a fragment of it. That is why "contains x" is written `*x*` and not `x`: without the two stars the criterion means the cell holds nothing but the single letter x. Case is ignored, so `tlal*` and `Tlal*` count the same. Accents are not ignored, so `Mexico` and `México` are two different strings.

## Exam routes used here

**MO-201 3.1.1, part A, AND, OR, NOT and COUNTIF.** Select the cell. Go to the **Formulas** tab, **Function Library** group. AND, OR and NOT are under **Logical**. COUNTIF is not in a top-level gallery: click **More Functions**, then **Statistical**, then **COUNTIF**. Click the function name and the **Function Arguments** dialog opens, titled with it. Click into the first argument box, read the description under the boxes, then type the reference or use the collapse button at the right of the box to drag the range. Press `Tab` to move on; Excel shows each argument's value beside its box and the running answer on the **Formula result =** line. Click **OK**. The boxes, in order: AND takes **Logical1**, **Logical2** and so on, OR the same, NOT takes a single **Logical**, and COUNTIF takes **Range** then **Criteria**.

**MO-201 3.1.1, part B, putting AND or OR inside an IF.** This is the part the exam is testing.

1. Build the IF first. **Formulas** tab, **Function Library** group, **Logical**, **IF**.
2. Click into the **Logical_test** box and leave it empty.
3. Look at the **Name Box**, at the left end of the formula bar. While a Function Arguments dialog is open it stops showing the cell reference and becomes a function drop-down.
4. Open it and pick **AND** or **OR** from the recently used list, or pick **More Functions...** to reach **Insert Function** and choose from **Or select a category:** and **Select a function:**.
5. The IF dialog is replaced by the AND or OR dialog. Fill **Logical1**, **Logical2** and any further boxes, and do not click OK. To come back out, click `IF` inside the formula bar; the IF dialog returns with the nested call already sitting in **Logical_test**.
6. Fill **Value_if_true** and **Value_if_false**, then click **OK** once, at the IF level. One OK commits the whole nest.

To check it, select the cell and press `Shift+F3`. If the nest was built cleanly, **Function Arguments** reopens on IF with every box filled and `AND(...)` showing inside **Logical_test**. If the whole formula appears inside the first box, it was typed as text.

**MO-200 4.2.3, the IF itself.** Click the result cell. **Formulas** tab, **Function Library** group, **Logical**, **IF**. Fill **Logical_test** with the comparison, **Value_if_true** with the text typed without quotation marks, because the dialog adds them and shows the finished value to the right of the box, and **Value_if_false** the same way. An empty **Value_if_false** returns FALSE, which is almost never what the task wants. Read **Formula result =**, click **OK**.

**MO-200 2.3.1, check and repair a defined range.** Go to the **Formulas** tab, **Defined Names** group, and click **Name Manager**. The list shows each name with its **Refers To**, its **Scope** and its comment. Select `Names`, click **Edit...**, and correct the **Refers to** box so it reads `=WILDCARDS!$A$2:$A$24`, using the collapse arrow at the right of the box to drag the range instead of typing it. Click **OK**, then **Close**. To prove the fix took, click the **Name Box** drop-down at the left of the formula bar and select the name: it jumps to and selects the range, and the header cell must now be outside the selection. `Ctrl+F3` opens Name Manager directly.

**MO-200 1.2.1, wildcards in Find.** Click any single cell; do not select a range first, because a multi-cell selection silently confines the search to it. Go to the **Home** tab, **Editing** group, click **Find & Select**, then **Find...**. In the **Find and Replace** dialog, on the **Find** tab, click **Options >>** to expand it. This click is the objective; the collapsed dialog cannot express anything that follows. Type the pattern in **Find what:**, for example `Tla*`. Set **Within:** to **Sheet** or **Workbook**, **Search:** to **By Rows** or **By Columns**, and **Look in:** to **Values** so the search reads what is displayed rather than the formulas behind it. Select **Match entire cell contents**, which is what makes the wildcards behave the way COUNTIF does. Click **Find All**: the dialog grows a result list of Book, Sheet, Name, Cell, Value and Formula for every hit, and the footer reports the count. `Ctrl+F` opens the same dialog collapsed, which is the short route and reaches none of the lists.

## Checks

`AND-OR`. The seven answer columns have a shape you can count without reading every cell:

| Column | Formula | TRUE in |
|---|---|---|
| E, AND (ALL) | `=AND(A2:D2)` | 1 row, the first |
| F, OR (ALL) | `=OR(A2:D2)` | 15 rows, all but the last |
| G, NOT | `=NOT(F2)` | 1 row, the last |
| H, AND 1, 2, 3 | `=AND(A2,B2,C2)` | 2 rows, the first two |
| I, OR 1, 2, 3 | `=OR(A2,B2,C2)` | 14 rows, all but the last two |
| J, AND 2, 4 | `=AND(B2,D2)` | 4 rows: 2, 4, 10 and 12 |
| K, OR 2, 4 | `=OR(B2,D2)` | 12 rows, false only in 7, 9, 15 and 17 |

Two sanity rules across the whole sheet. An AND column can never have more TRUE rows than the OR column built from the same inputs, so E cannot beat F, H cannot beat I, and J cannot beat K. And J and K are the two-input truth table hidden inside the four-input one: over sixteen rows AND of two inputs must come out TRUE a quarter of the time, four rows, and OR three quarters of the time, twelve rows.

## Notes on the source

- All three sheets ship at least partly solved. `AND-OR` has E, F and G written; `IF-AND-OR` has all nine answers written; `WILDCARDS` has all nine COUNTIFs written. Clear H2:K17, the nine answer cells, and E2:E10 before handing the file to students, or work from the CSV files above.
- The instructions call the first sheet the "YES-OR-NO" sheet. The sheet is called `AND-OR`. The Spanish original names it after the three Spanish function names, Y, O and NO, and the translation read the Y as "yes".
- Column G on `AND-OR` is headed `NO(ALL)` and holds `=NOT(F2)`, which is NOT applied to the OR of the four inputs, not NOT applied to each input. It answers "none of the four is true", which is a real and useful thing to compute and is not what the header promises.
- Question 2 on `IF-AND-OR` asks whether José's hair is Brown and tests B8, the hair row. The three hair values on that sheet are Blond, Brunette and Bold; Brown is an eye colour, one row up. The formula is therefore always FALSE and the answer is always "Other". It still works as an exercise, and a student who notices is reading the data properly. `Bold` in D8 is Bald.
- The nine answers on `IF-AND-OR` sit in nine unrelated cells, G11, F13, H15, K17, J19, J21, J23, K25 and B28, with no pattern to them. Keep yours in one column.
- The defined name `Names` covers A1:A24, which includes the header cell. See the check above for why that happens to be harmless here and should still be fixed.
- The instructions describe wildcards as usable "directly in the SEARCH box". That is the **Find what:** box of **Find and Replace**, which is why task 16 exists.
- Not from `procedures.en.md`, so confirm it on the lab build before teaching it: a literal asterisk or question mark is matched by putting a tilde in front of it, `~*`. Nothing in this exercise needs it, and a student will hit it the first time a product code contains a star.
