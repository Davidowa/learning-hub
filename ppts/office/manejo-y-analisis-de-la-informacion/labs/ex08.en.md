# Exercise 08 · Nested IF

Three sheets, three questions with three possible answers each, and one function that has to be put inside itself to reach the third answer. A football round where each result is a win, a draw or a loss; a dog show where the prize money depends on the placing; and a month of tuition payments where the amount owed depends on the day the parent pays. It lands in week 7, sessions 13 and 14, where the syllabus schedules nested IF, and it is the file the Evaluate Formula tool was put in that session for. Hand in the workbook with column E filled on `Ej1`, column D on `Ej2`, and columns B and C on `Ej3`.

**This exercise ships without a .docx.** Every other exercise in the set carries its instructions in a Word file; this folder holds only the workbook. What follows was reconstructed from the workbook itself: the seventy-two formulas already recorded in the three sheets, and the notes the author left in the cells beside them, G2:G4 on `Ej1` and F2:F4 on `Ej2`. Where the workbook is silent, the reconstruction says so rather than inventing a requirement.

**Objectives** MO-200 4.2.3, MO-200 2.1.2, MO-200 2.3.1, MO-200 4.1.2, MO-201 3.1.1, MO-201 3.5.4

## What to do

**On `Ej1`.**

1. In E2:E6 report the group 1 team's result in three states, using one IF nested inside another. The outer test settles the win, the inner test separates the draw from the loss: `=IF(B2>D2,"Ganó",IF(B2=D2,"Empató","Perdió"))`. Route MO-201 3.1.1, part B. Keep the three strings exactly as the workbook writes them, or translate all three and say you did; do not mix languages inside the column.

**On `Ej2`.**

2. In D2:D7 calculate the new value from the placing, again with one nesting: `=IF(C2=1,B2*2,IF(C2<4,B2+300,B2+100))`. Note what the inner test does. Once the outer IF has ruled out first place, `C2<4` can only mean second or third, so the nested form needs two tests where a written-out rule needs three. That is the whole lesson of the sheet.

**On `Ej3`.**

3. Name the three constants before writing the formula. Select C8 and define a name for the tuition, F8 for the discount, F9 for the fee. Route MO-200 2.3.1. The workbook ships with them defined as `Tuit`, `Disc` and `Fee`.
4. Fill the day numbers into B12:B42. Type 1 in B12, select B12:B42, and use Fill and the Series dialog with **Type** set to **Linear** and **Step value** 1. Route MO-200 2.1.2. The shipped workbook does this a different way, with `=+B12+1` in B13 filled down, which works and is a habit worth unlearning; see the notes.
5. In C12:C42 calculate the amount owed on that day, three bands with one nesting: `=IF(B12<=4,Tuit*(1-Disc),IF(B12<=10,Tuit,Tuit*(1+Fee)))`. Pay on or before the 4th and the discount applies. Pay from the 5th to the 10th and the tuition is the plain amount. Pay after the 10th and the fee is added. Route MO-201 3.1.1, part B, with the names coming from MO-200 4.1.2.
6. Open **Evaluate Formula** on C12 and step through it until the dialog reduces the formula to a single number. Route MO-201 3.5.4. Do the same on C20 and C40, so you watch the formula take each of its three branches in turn.

## Exam routes used here

**MO-200 4.2.3, the outer IF.** Click the result cell. Go to the **Formulas** tab, **Function Library** group, click **Logical**, and click **IF**. In the **Function Arguments** dialog fill **Logical_test** with the comparison, then **Value_if_true** with the text, typed without quotation marks because the dialog adds them. Read **Formula result =** at the bottom. Leave **Value_if_false** for the nested call. Shortcut: `Shift+F3` inside an existing IF reopens **Function Arguments** on that formula.

**MO-201 3.1.1, part B, nesting one function inside another.** This is the part the exam is testing, and typing the formula never proves it.

1. Build the outer IF with the route above, up to the point where **Value_if_false** is the box that needs the nested call.
2. Click into **Value_if_false** and leave it empty.
3. Look at the **Name Box**, at the left end of the formula bar. While a Function Arguments dialog is open it stops showing the cell reference and becomes a function drop-down.
4. Open it and pick **IF** from the list of recently used functions, or pick **More Functions...** to reach **Insert Function** and choose from **Or select a category:** and **Select a function:**.
5. The outer dialog is replaced by the inner IF's own dialog. Fill its three boxes and do not click OK yet. To come back out, click the outer function's name inside the formula bar; the outer dialog returns with the nested call already in place.
6. Click **OK** once, at the outer level. One OK commits the whole nest.

To check it, select the cell and press `Shift+F3` again. If the nest was built cleanly, **Function Arguments** reopens on the outer IF with every box filled. If it opens with the whole formula sitting inside the first box, the nest was typed as text and a grader that re-parses it will fail it.

**MO-201 3.5.4, Evaluate Formula.** Select the single cell holding the formula; the tool works on one cell at a time. Go to the **Formulas** tab, **Formula Auditing** group, and click **Evaluate Formula**. **Reference:** at the top shows the cell fully qualified, for example `Ej3!$C$12`. **Evaluation:** below shows the formula with one part underlined. Click **Evaluate**: the underlined part is replaced by its result in italic, and the underline moves to the next part. Keep clicking until the formula is a single value. When the underlined part points at a cell that itself holds a formula, **Step In** lights up; click it to open that cell's formula indented below, then **Step Out** to carry the resolved value back. Click **Close**; the dialog has no OK because it changes nothing on the sheet. The short route is to select a fragment in the formula bar and press `F9`, which replaces it with its value on the spot, and which must be escaped with `Esc` and never `Enter`, because Enter writes the hard-coded value into the formula permanently.

**MO-200 2.3.1, define a named range.** Select the cell. **Formulas** tab, **Defined Names** group, **Define Name**. Type the name in **Name**, set **Scope** to **Workbook**, check that **Refers to** holds an absolute reference such as `='Ej3'!$C$8`, click **OK**. `Ctrl+F3` opens **Name Manager**, which is where you rename or repoint one later.

**MO-200 2.1.2, Auto Fill.** Type 1 in B12. Select B12 together with B13:B42. Go to the **Home** tab, **Editing** group, click **Fill**, then **Series...**. Set **Series in** to **Columns**, **Type** to **Linear**, **Step value** to 1. Click **OK**.

## Notes on the source

- **No instructions file.** The folder holds `Excel 8 (Nested IF).xlsx` and nothing else. Everything above under "What to do" was inferred from the recorded formulas and from the two blocks of author notes inside the sheets. Two things could not be inferred and are not required here: which of the three sheets is meant to be attempted first, and whether the sheets were ever meant to be formatted.
- **The workbook ships solved.** All seventy-two formulas are already in place, so there is nothing left for a student to do until E2:E6, D2:D7, B13:B42 and C12:C42 are cleared. `COBERTURA.md` counts eighty-four formulas in this file; the count in the file is seventy-two, five on `Ej1`, six on `Ej2` and sixty-one on `Ej3`.
- `Ej1` returns Spanish strings, `Ganó`, `Empató` and `Perdió`, and its author notes in G2:G4 are in Spanish, while `Ej2` returns nothing but numbers and its notes in F2:F4 are in English. The workbook was assembled from two sources and never made consistent.
- `Ej3` builds its day column with `=+B12+1` filled down. The leading plus sign is a Lotus 1-2-3 habit that Excel accepts and discards; it does nothing. It also makes the day column a chain of formulas, so deleting one cell breaks every cell below it. Fill Series is the better route and the one the certification asks about.
- `Ej3` labels the month `MAY` in B10 with no year, and gives it thirty-one days, which May has. Nothing in the sheet depends on the month being May.
- Spelling in the shipped workbook: `Rottwhiller` for Rottweiler, `Pittbull` for Pitbull, and `Pabli Podestá` for Pablo Podestá, which is a district of Buenos Aires province, as are most of the other team names.
- The tuition in C8 and the amounts in C12:C42 carry no currency format. Adding one is not part of the exercise as recorded, and it would be a reasonable extension since the number is money.
