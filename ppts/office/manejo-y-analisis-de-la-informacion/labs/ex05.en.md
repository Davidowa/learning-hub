# Exercise 5 · Operator precedence, references and defined names

Eighteen small arithmetic expressions and a hardware shop's ledger. The first sheet is there to prove that Excel does not read left to right the way a person does, and that a pair of brackets changes the answer. The second is where relative references get filled down a column, one absolute reference gets pinned with `F4`, and a defined name gets used as a third kind of absolute. It lands in week 3, session 6, paired with Exercise 2. Hand in the finished workbook as `Exercise 5 Excel_Your name.xlsx`.

**Objectives** MO-200 2.3.1, 4.1.1, 4.1.2, 4.2.1; MO-201 3.5.1, 3.5.2 in the optional extension

## Operator precedence

The instruction document introduces this table and then leaves it empty, a one-cell table with nothing in it. What follows is the standard Excel order, reconstructed, highest precedence first. Verify it against the product before it goes to the cohort.

| Order | Operator | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `:` `space` `,` | Reference operators: range, intersection, union |
| 2 | `-` | Negation, as in `-5` |
| 3 | `%` | Percent |
| 4 | `^` | Exponentiation |
| 5 | `*` and `/` | Multiplication and division, equal rank, left to right |
| 6 | `+` and `-` | Addition and subtraction, equal rank, left to right |
| 7 | `&` | Text concatenation |
| 8 | `=` `<` `>` `<=` `>=` `<>` | Comparison |

Operators of equal rank are evaluated left to right. Brackets override all of it, and brackets inside brackets are worked from the inside out.

Two words the exercise uses precisely. A **formula** is an equation the user writes, such as `=B7*D7`. A **function** is a predefined formula that takes arguments, such as `=SUM(I7:I20)`. Both start with `=`.

## What to do

1. On the `Precedence` sheet, work out all eighteen expressions by typing each one into its Answer cell with a leading `=`. B2 gets `=5+4*3`, E2 gets `=(5+4)*3`, H2 gets `=5+(4*3)`, and so on down to row 7.
2. Compare the three answers in each row. Rows 1, 3 and 6 give the same result twice and something different once. Rows 2, 4 and 5 give three different pairs. The brackets in column G never change the answer, because they only make explicit what Excel was going to do anyway.
3. On the `Earnings` sheet, fill in the six calculated columns. Write each formula once in row 7 and fill it down to row 20.
4. **Total Cost**, F7: `=B7*D7`. Quantity times unit cost. Relative references, so filling down moves both of them one row at a time. Route 4.1.1.
5. **Total Revenue**, G7: `=B7*E7`. Quantity times unit price.
6. **Discount**, H7: `=G7*$B$4`. Total revenue times the discount rate. B4 is one cell above the table and it has to stay B4 for all fourteen rows, so it becomes absolute. Type the reference, leave the insertion point touching it, and press `F4`. Route 4.1.1.
7. **Net Revenue**, I7: `=G7-H7`. Total revenue less the discount.
8. **Earnings**, J7: `=I7-F7`. Net revenue less total cost.
9. **% Earnings**, K7: `=J7/I7`. Earnings over net revenue.
10. Name cell B3 `VAT`, either from **Formulas** > **Defined Names** > **Define Name** or by typing into the Name Box. Route 2.3.1. A defined name behaves as an absolute reference wherever it is used, which is the third way to pin a cell after `$B$4` and `F4`.
11. In I22, next to the `SUBTOTAL` label, total the net revenue with the SUM function: `=SUM(I7:I20)`. Route 4.2.1.
12. In I23, next to the `VAT` label, work out the tax on that subtotal using the name from task 10: `=I22*VAT`. Route 4.1.2.
13. In I24, next to the `TOTAL` label, add the two: `=I22+I23`.
14. In I25, next to the `TOTAL EARNINGS` label, total the earnings column: `=SUM(J7:J20)`.
15. Save as `Exercise 5 Excel_Your name.xlsx` and upload it to Moodle.

## Exam routes used here

**2.3.1 Define a named range.** Select B3. **Formulas** tab, **Defined Names** group, **Define Name**. The **New Name** dialog opens. Type `VAT` into the **Name** box: a name starts with a letter, an underscore or a backslash, carries no spaces, and cannot be a cell address such as `A1` or a single letter `C` or `R`. Excel is not case sensitive here, so `VAT` and `vat` are one name. Open **Scope** and pick **Workbook**, so the name works from any sheet, or a sheet name to limit it. Fill **Comment** if the task asks for a description. Check that **Refers to** holds an absolute reference, `=Earnings!$B$3`; the collapse arrow at the right of the box reselects on the sheet. Click **OK**. To name several ranges from their labels at once, select the block including its header row and use **Create from Selection**, ticking **Top row**, **Left column**, **Bottom row** or **Right column**. To edit or delete, use **Name Manager** in the same group, with **New...**, **Edit...**, **Delete** and **Filter**. The Name Box is the short route: it creates the name, always scopes it to Workbook, offers no Comment, and never shows the **Refers to** it built, so there is no chance to catch a wrong range. `Ctrl+F3` opens Name Manager, `F3` opens **Paste Name** inside a formula, `Alt, M, M, D` opens Define Name.

**4.1.1 Insert relative, absolute, and mixed references.** Click the cell that will hold the formula and type `=`. Click the cell you want to refer to, or type its address; it arrives relative, as `B4`. Leave the insertion point touching that reference and press `F4`. Each press cycles one step: `B4`, `$B$4`, `B$4`, `$B4`, back to `B4`. Stop on the form the task needs. `$B$4` locks column and row, which is what the discount rate needs. `B$4` locks the row only, so the reference slides sideways when filled right and stays on row 4 when filled down. `$B4` locks the column only. Press `Enter`, then fill in both directions with the fill handle or `Ctrl+R` and `Ctrl+D`, and read the formula bar in the far corner of the filled block to confirm the locked part did not move. Typing the dollar signs by hand is honest here, because the exam grades the reference that ends up in the cell rather than the key that produced it. What costs marks is dodging the problem by writing one formula per row, which produces the right numbers and fails a task worded "write one formula and fill it down". On laptops with a function lock, `Fn+F4`.

**4.1.2 Reference named ranges and named tables in formulas.** Create the name first, as in 2.3.1. Inside the formula, start typing the first letters of the name: Formula AutoComplete lists it with a tag icon, and `Tab` inserts it. Or press `F3` to open the **Paste Name** dialog, select the name, click **OK**. So I23 is typed as `=I22*` and then `VAT` picked from the list, which gives `=I22*VAT`. Dragging over the cell with the mouse so the formula fills with `$B$3` is the short route: the arithmetic is identical, and when the task says "use the defined name" the address is the wrong answer. Read the formula bar afterwards, it has to show the name and not an address.

**4.2.1 Perform calculations by using the AVERAGE(), MAX(), MIN(), and SUM() functions.** From the ribbon: click I22, put the result cell directly under the column being summarised so Excel guesses well, then go to the **Formulas** tab, **Function Library** group, and click the arrow under **AutoSum**. Pick **Sum**, **Average**, **Max**, **Min** or **Count Numbers**; the plain button face applies Sum. Excel writes the function and proposes a range with a moving dashed border, and dragging over I7:I20 while it is still open replaces the argument. Press `Enter`. Through the dialog: click I22, **Formulas** > **Function Library** > **Insert Function**, open **Or select a category** and pick **Math & Trig** for SUM, select it in **Select a function**, read the syntax line, click **OK**. In **Function Arguments**, click into **Number1** and drag over I7:I20, using the collapse button at the right of the box if the dialog covers the data, then read **Formula result =** at the bottom left before clicking **OK**. Typing `=SUM(I7:I20)` straight into the cell, or reading the total off the status bar, is right and shows no route. `Alt+=` inserts SUM with a guessed range, `Shift+F3` opens **Insert Function**.

**Expert 3.5.1 Trace precedence and dependence.** Select the cell holding the formula. **Formulas** tab, **Formula Auditing** group, **Trace Precedents**. Blue arrows appear, running from every cell the formula reads into the selected cell, with a dot at each source end. Click again to go one level further back; each click adds a level, and you keep clicking until no new arrow appears. A dashed black arrow pointing at a small worksheet icon means the precedent is on another sheet or in another workbook: double-click that arrow to open **Go To**, which lists the external reference. **Trace Dependents** in the same group does the reverse, the cells that read this one. To clear, click the arrow on **Remove Arrows** and choose **Remove Arrows**, **Remove Precedent Arrows** or **Remove Dependent Arrows**; the exam checks that the sheet was left clean. To select the feeding cells rather than draw arrows, use **Go To Special** and choose **Precedents** or **Dependents**, with **Direct only** or **All levels** underneath. `Ctrl+[` selects the direct precedents and `Ctrl+]` the direct dependents, which answers a "select" item and fails a "trace" item, because they draw nothing.

**Expert 3.5.2 Monitor cells and formulas by using the Watch Window.** **Formulas** tab, **Formula Auditing** group, **Watch Window**. A pane opens, docked or floating. Click **Add Watch...**, select the cell or range on the sheet so the reference box fills with the full reference including the sheet name, and click **Add**. The watch appears as a row under six columns: **Book**, **Sheet**, **Name**, **Cell**, **Value**, **Formula**. The Name column stays empty unless the cell carries a defined name. Double-click any row to jump to that cell wherever it is, click a column heading to sort, and select a row plus **Delete Watch** to remove one. Closing the pane does not delete the watches; reopening it brings them back, which is what proves a watch was added rather than a value merely looked at. There is no keyboard shortcut for either the pane or Add Watch.

## Known problems in the source

- The operator table in the instruction document is a one-cell table with nothing in it. The picture that belonged there is gone. The table at the top of this file is a reconstruction and needs checking against the product.
- The instruction sheet says "% Profit = Profit / Net Income" and the sheet header says `% Earnings`. Same column, two names. The formula is the same either way.
- The instruction sheet calls I22 the total income obtained, while the label on the sheet reads `SUBTOTAL`. It is the sum of the net revenue column, which is a subtotal in the sense that VAT has not been added yet.
- The title in A1 reads `FERRETERÍA  MÉXICO` with two spaces between the words.
- Nothing in this exercise needs a number format applied, and the workbook already carries Accounting on the unit cost and unit price columns and a whole-percent format on B3 and B4. The calculated columns come out with the General format, so the currency columns and `% Earnings` will look wrong until someone formats them. The instructions never mention it.
