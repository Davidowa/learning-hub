# Exercise 22 · Automatic subtotals and outline levels

Five copies of the same 576 rows, four of them carrying a different automatic subtotal. The student sorts, runs the **Subtotal** dialog once per sheet, and hands back a workbook where each copy collapses to its own summary. What makes it worth the repetition is that the four passes use four different break columns and three different summary functions, so the habit of sorting first gets drilled four times instead of explained once. It lands in week 14, sessions 27 and 28, the week the syllabus gives to subtotals and PivotTables.

**Objectives** MO-200 3.3.2, MO-201 2.2.3, MO-201 2.2.4

## The data

One sheet, called `Datos` in the shipped workbook, holding population counts for Mexico City. Four columns, 576 rows of data in `A2:D577`, headers in row 1.

| Column | Heading | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| A | TOWN | 16 boroughs, 36 rows each |
| B | GENDER | Male and Female, 288 rows each |
| C | AGE RANGE | 18 bands, `a) 0 a 4` through `r) 85 and more`, 32 rows each |
| D | NUMBER | inhabitants, whole numbers from 387 to 76,092 |

Sixteen boroughs by two genders by eighteen age bands is exactly 576 rows, so the grid is complete and every group has the same shape. The grand total is 9,200,318 inhabitants.

The file is [`labs/data/ex22-population.csv`](data/ex22-population.csv), 576 rows plus a header, UTF-8.

The rows arrive sorted ascending by NUMBER, which is the one order that is useless for every task below. That is deliberate. Subtotals on unsorted data produce one group per row, and the fastest way to learn it is to see it happen.

## What to do

1. Rename the sheet to `Data`. The shipped workbook calls it `Datos`, and the rest of the instructions use the English name.
2. Make four copies of it. Right-click the sheet tab, click **Move or Copy**, select **Create a copy**, click **OK**, four times. Excel names them `Data (2)`, `Data (3)`, `Data (4)` and `Data (5)`.
3. On `Data (2)`, sum the inhabitants grouped by TOWN. Sort by TOWN first. Route: MO-200 3.3.2 for the sort, MO-201 2.2.4 for the subtotal.
4. On `Data (3)`, average the inhabitants grouped by AGE RANGE. Sort by AGE RANGE first. The letter prefix on each band, `a)` through `r)`, exists so that an A to Z sort puts the bands in age order rather than alphabetically by number.
5. On `Data (4)`, sum the inhabitants grouped by GENDER. Sort by GENDER first.
6. On `Data (5)`, take the maximum number of inhabitants grouped by TOWN. Sort by TOWN first.
7. On each of the four sheets, collapse the outline to level 2 so only the subtotal rows show, then to level 1 so only the grand total shows, then back to level 3. Route: MO-201 2.2.3.
8. Leave the original `Data` sheet with no subtotals on it, as the untouched source.

## Exam routes used here

**Sort by a column. MO-200 3.3.2**

1. Click any single cell inside the range. Do not preselect one column, which is how the rows get torn apart.
2. **Data** tab, **Sort & Filter** group, click **Sort**.
3. The **Sort** dialog opens. Select the **My data has headers** check box, so row 1 stays put and the lists show TOWN, GENDER, AGE RANGE and NUMBER instead of A, B, C and D.
4. Open the **Sort by** list and pick the column that will break the groups.
5. Leave **Sort On** at **Cell Values** and **Order** at **A to Z**.
6. Click **OK**.

Clicking in the column and using **Home** tab, **Editing** group, **Sort & Filter**, **Sort A to Z** reaches the same arrangement and leaves no sort levels behind for anyone to read. Not graded.

**Insert subtotals. MO-201 2.2.4**

1. Sort the list by the column that will break the groups, as above. Subtotals on unsorted data produce one group per row.
2. Click a single cell inside the list. Do not select the range. Excel expands to the whole block on its own.
3. **Data** tab, **Outline** group, click **Subtotal**.
4. In the **Subtotal** dialog, open the **At each change in** list and select the column you just sorted by.
5. Open the **Use function** list and select **Sum**, **Average** or **Max**. The list also offers Count, Min, Product, Count Numbers, StdDev, StdDevp, Var and Varp.
6. In the **Add subtotal to** list, tick **NUMBER** and clear everything else. Excel preselects the last numeric column, which happens to be right here and is rarely right elsewhere, so check it rather than trusting it.
7. Leave **Replace current subtotals** selected. Clear it only when you are stacking a second function on top of a first, otherwise the second pass deletes the first.
8. Leave **Summary below data** selected.
9. Click **OK**. Excel inserts rows carrying `SUBTOTAL` formulas and builds a three-level outline down the left margin.
10. To take them out again: click a cell in the list, reopen the dialog, click **Remove All**.

Typing `=SUM()` under each group by hand gives the right arithmetic, no outline, and a grand total that double-counts unless you dodge the subtotal rows one by one. Not graded.

**Use the outline. MO-201 2.2.3**

1. The outline bar sits in a margin to the left of the row headers, with numbered level buttons 1, 2 and 3 above it.
2. Click **2** to show only the subtotal rows and the grand total. Click **1** for the grand total alone. Click **3** to bring the detail back.
3. The minus and plus buttons on the bar collapse and expand one group at a time.
4. **Show Detail** and **Hide Detail** in the **Data** tab, **Outline** group act on whichever group the cursor is in.
5. Ctrl+8 shows and hides the outline symbols. That is also the fastest proof that an outline exists at all.
6. To remove the outline without removing the subtotals, click the **Ungroup** arrow in the **Data** tab, **Outline** group, and click **Clear Outline**.

Selecting rows and clicking **Hide** on the row header looks identical on screen and leaves nothing that says these rows belong together. Not graded.

## Checks

Click any subtotal cell and read the formula bar. It has to start with `SUBTOTAL`, not `SUM`. Sum writes `=SUBTOTAL(9,...)`, Average writes `=SUBTOTAL(1,...)`, Max writes `=SUBTOTAL(4,...)`.

Press Ctrl+8 on each of the four copies. The outline bar vanishes and returns. Hidden rows never do that.

`Data (2)`, sum by TOWN. Sixteen subtotal rows plus a grand total of **9,200,318**. Iztapalapa is the largest at **1,835,091** and Milpa Alta the smallest at **152,671**. Collapse to level 2 and the sheet shows seventeen rows.

`Data (3)`, average by AGE RANGE. Eighteen subtotal rows, each averaging 32 detail rows. Band `a) 0 a 4` averages **14,997.00** exactly, band `f) 25 a 29` peaks at **23,509.03**, and band `r) 85 and more` bottoms out at **3,295.94**. The grand total row shows **15,972.77**, which is the average of all 576 detail rows and not the average of the eighteen subtotals. Those two numbers happen to agree here because every band holds the same 32 rows; on uneven groups they would not, and the difference is the point.

`Data (4)`, sum by GENDER. Two subtotal rows: Female **4,800,174**, Male **4,400,144**. They add to the same 9,200,318.

`Data (5)`, max by TOWN. Sixteen subtotal rows. Iztapalapa **76,092**, Milpa Alta **6,977**, and a grand total of **76,092**, which is the largest single value in the whole column and not the sum of the sixteen maxima.

Last check, on the sorting. On `Data (3)`, collapse to level 2 and read the eighteen band labels down the sheet. They run `a)` to `r)` in order. If they run in some other order, the sort was done on NUMBER or skipped, and the subtotal rows are counting the wrong things even though every formula in them is spelled correctly.

## Notes on the source

The sheet is named `Datos` in the workbook while the instructions call it `Data`. Task 1 above closes the gap; without it, the four copies come out named `Datos (2)` through `Datos (5)`.

The AGE RANGE labels are half translated. They read `a) 0 a 4`, with the Spanish `a` for "to", while the last one reads `r) 85 and more` in English. Several also carry two trailing spaces. The CSV keeps all of it exactly as the sheet holds it, because changing the label text changes what the groups are and would put this file out of step with the professor's workbook. Worth fixing in the source before the next cohort, and worth leaving alone until then.
