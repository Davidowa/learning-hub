# Exercise 16 · Excel tables and structured references

A clothing distributor keeps thirty orders and wants to read its shipping logistics at a glance. The work is to turn a plain range into a real Excel table, write both calculated columns as structured references rather than cell addresses, colour the shipment status with a rule, and close the table with a configured total row. It lands in week 10, session 19, which COBERTURA calls the heaviest closing week of the plan, and it is the file the syllabus line "apply table format, name a table, banded rows, total rows" was written for. Hand in one workbook holding one named table, two calculated columns, two conditional formats and a total row.

**Objectives** MO-200 2.1.1, 2.3.2, 2.4.2, 3.1.1, 3.1.2, 3.2.1, 3.2.2, 3.2.3, 4.1.2, MO-201 3.1.1

Source workbook `Excel 16.xlsx`. Thirty orders in eight columns, of which four are given and four you build. Order Date, Order Month, Quantity and Status are missing from the CSV files on purpose: two of them you generate with RANDBETWEEN and two you calculate.

### Status key

[data/ex16-status-key.csv](data/ex16-status-key.csv), 5 rows. It sits at L2:N7 of the `Exercise1` sheet, beside the data, and it is the rulebook for both the IFS formula and the colours.

| Destination | Status | Color |
|---|---|---|
| NUEVO LEON | Sent | Yellow |
| GUANAJUATO | In progress | Blue |
| VERACRUZ | Delivered | Green |
| SINALOA | Payment pending | Red |
| DURANGO | Payment pending | REd |

Two things worth noticing before you start. `REd` in the last row is a typo for `Red`, kept here as the source has it. The key gives no status for any destination outside those five, so a sixth destination would come back as an error rather than as blank. And the Origin column holds two values that break its own pattern, `PLANTA NORTE C` on order 2021-27 and `DORADO` on order 2021-29. Neither breaks anything in this exercise, and both are exactly the kind of dirt that breaks a lookup later.

## What to do

1. Fill the Order Date column with random dates between 1 January and 30 June 2023. Those two dates are the serial numbers 44927 and 45107, so the formula is `=RANDBETWEEN(44927,45107)`. Format the column as **Short Date** or the dates stay five-digit numbers. Route 2.2.5.
2. Copy the dates and paste them back over themselves as values. Route 2.1.1. RANDBETWEEN is volatile: every edit anywhere in the workbook redraws all thirty dates, so skipping this makes the exercise unrepeatable.
3. Fill the Quantity column with random values between 100 and 500, `=RANDBETWEEN(100,500)`, then paste those as values too.
4. Write the Order Month column as `=MONTH([@[Order Date]])`. Write it once in the first data row and let the calculated column fill itself down. The reference has to be structured, not `=MONTH(C3)`. Route 4.1.2.
5. Write the Status column with IFS, reading the destination and returning the status from the key. Route Expert 3.1.1.

   ```
   =IFS([@Destination]="Nuevo Leon","Sent",
        [@Destination]="Guanajuato","In progress",
        [@Destination]="Veracruz","Delivered",
        OR([@Destination]="Sinaloa",[@Destination]="Durango"),"Payment pending")
   ```

   The comparison is case insensitive, so `"Nuevo Leon"` matches the `NUEVO LEON` in the cells. The two states that share a status are folded into one OR test rather than into two more IFS pairs.
6. Colour the Status column with a conditional format, one rule per status, through **Highlight Cells Rules** and **Text that Contains**. Yellow for Sent, blue for In progress, green for Delivered, red for Payment pending. Route 2.4.2. Do not fill the cells by hand.
7. Put data bars on the Order Month column. Route 2.4.2 again, through the **Data Bars** gallery.
8. Convert the range of order information into a table. Route 3.1.1. Use the **Insert** tab route so that the range Excel proposes can be read before it is accepted, then pick a style from the gallery. Route 3.1.2.
9. Append the eight orders from `Ex1-2` to the bottom of the table. Route 3.2.1. The Order Month and Status formulas have to fill down on their own; if they do not, those columns were never calculated columns.
10. Turn on the total row and set **Count** on the Status column and **Average** on the Quantity column. Route 3.2.3. Every other total cell stays at **None**.
11. Turn on **Banded Columns** in Table Style Options and leave **Banded Rows** on. Route 3.2.2.
12. Name the table with your own first name, through the **Table Name** box on the **Table Design** tab. Route 2.3.2.

## Exam routes used here

**3.1.1 Create Excel tables from cell ranges.** Click one single cell inside the block of data, do not drag the range by hand. **Insert** tab, **Tables** group, **Table**. The **Create Table** dialog opens; read the address under **Where is the data for your table?** and check that it covers the header row and the last row of data. Select the **My table has headers** check box. Click **OK**. The contextual **Table Design** tab must appear, the table takes the name `Table1` and the default style Blue, Table Style Medium 2, and filter buttons appear in the header row. `Ctrl+T` opens the same dialog, and pressing `Ctrl+T` then `Enter` accepts Excel's guess without reading it, which is the habit that loses the item when the data touches a stray cell.

**3.1.2 Apply table styles.** Click inside the table, go to **Table Design**, **Table Styles** group, and click the **More** arrow at the bottom right of the gallery to open the whole set. The gallery is split into **Light**, **Medium** and **Dark**. Point at a style and read the tooltip, which gives the literal name, for example "Green, Table Style Medium 7". Click the one the task names. If the table still shows Blue, Table Style Medium 2, nothing was applied, because that is what Excel gives on creation.

**2.3.2 Name a table.** Click any cell inside the table. The **Table Design** tab holds the **Properties** group at its left end, and that group holds the box labelled **Table Name:**. Click in the box, select the whole of the existing name, type the new one, and press **Enter**. Nothing is committed until Enter is pressed; clicking away can discard it. The name follows the defined-name rules: start with a letter or an underscore, no spaces, not a cell address, unique across the workbook. Renaming through **Formulas** > **Name Manager** also works and is objective 2.3.1, not this one.

**4.1.2 Reference named ranges and named tables in formulas.** Inside the table, this row's cell in a column is written `[@Column]`; Excel drops the table name because the formula is already in the table, and `@` means this row. Outside the table the same column is `Lorena[Column]`. A column's header text is `Lorena[[#Headers],[Order Date]]`, and the other special items are `[#Data]`, `[#Totals]` and `[#All]`. Dragging over the cells with the mouse so the formula fills with `$C$3:$C$32` gives identical arithmetic and answers a different question. Read the formula bar afterwards: it has to show the column name, not an address.

**3.2.1 Add or remove table rows and columns.** To insert inside the table, right-click a cell in the row next to where the new one goes, point to **Insert**, and pick from the four table commands the submenu offers, **Table Columns to the Left**, **Table Column to the Right**, **Table Rows Above**, **Table Row Below**. The new row inherits the banding, the header naming and any calculated column formula. To resize the whole table in one move, **Table Design** tab, **Properties** group, **Resize Table**, then drag or type the new address under **Select the new data range for your table**; the header row has to stay in the same row. Typing into the first empty row under the table also works, through auto expansion, and demonstrates nothing.

**3.2.2 Configure table style options.** Click any cell inside the table. **Table Design** tab, **Table Style Options** group. Seven check boxes, all of them literal: **Header Row**, **Total Row**, **Banded Rows**, **First Column**, **Last Column**, **Banded Columns**, **Filter Button**. Select or clear every one the task names before leaving the group, because each is a separate graded action. Shading alternate rows by hand from **Home** > **Font** > **Fill Color** looks identical and breaks the moment a row is inserted.

**3.2.3 Insert and configure total rows.** **Table Design** tab, **Table Style Options** group, select the **Total Row** check box. A row appears at the bottom of the table, its first cell reads `Total`, and its last column already carries `=SUBTOTAL(109,[Status])`. Click the total cell under the column you need, click the drop-down arrow that appears on its right edge, and pick from **None**, **Average**, **Count**, **Count Numbers**, **Max**, **Min**, **Sum**, **StdDev**, **Var**, **More Functions**. Count writes `=SUBTOTAL(103,[Status])`, Average writes `=SUBTOTAL(101,[Quantity])`. Set **None** on every column that must stay empty. Typing `=SUM(E3:E32)` under the table gives the right number and is not a total row: it ignores filters and it slides out of alignment when a row is inserted. `Ctrl+Shift+T` is reported to toggle the total row, **TO CONFIRM** on the lab machines.

**2.4.2 Apply built-in conditional formatting.** Select the data cells only. Including the header row is the commonest cause of a rule that formats the wrong cells. **Home** tab, **Styles** group, **Conditional Formatting**. For the status colours, point at **Highlight Cells Rules** and click **Text that Contains...**; type the status in the left box, then open the **with** list on the right and pick a preset such as **Yellow Fill with Dark Yellow Text**, or click **Custom Format...**, which opens Format Cells cut down to four tabs, **Number**, **Font**, **Border** and **Fill**. For the month column, point at **Data Bars** and click a variant, or click **More Rules...** at the bottom of that gallery to reach **Show Bar Only** and thresholds typed as **Number**, **Percent**, **Formula** or **Percentile**. Ribbon keys **Alt, H, L**.

**2.1.1 Paste data by using special paste options.** Select the source range. **Home** tab, **Clipboard** group, **Copy**; do not use `Ctrl+X`, because cut disables most of the Paste Special options. Select the destination, which for this exercise is the same range. **Home** tab, **Clipboard** group, click the **arrow under the Paste button**, not the button face, then **Paste Special...** at the bottom of the gallery. In the **Paste** section select **Values**. Click **OK**, then press **Esc** to clear the marching border. `Ctrl+Alt+V` opens the dialog directly and `V` then `Enter` inside it pastes values.

**Expert 3.1.1 Perform logical operations by using nested functions.** The graded route does not type the function. Select the cell, **Formulas** tab, **Function Library** group, **Logical**, **IFS**. The **Function Arguments** dialog opens with **Logical_test1**, **Value_if_true1**, **Logical_test2**, **Value_if_true2** and so on, growing a new pair of boxes as the last one is filled. To nest the OR inside the fourth **Logical_test**, leave that box empty, look at the **Name Box** at the left end of the formula bar, which stops showing the cell reference and becomes a function drop-down while the dialog is open, and pick **OR** from it. Fill the two **Logical** boxes of OR, then click the word `IFS` in the formula bar to come back out to the outer dialog with the nested call already in place. Click **OK** once, at the outer level. One OK commits the whole nest.

## Checks

- Click inside the data and the **Table Design** tab appears. Open **Formulas** > **Defined Names** > **Name Manager**: the table is listed under your name with a table icon rather than a range icon.
- The table holds thirty data rows plus a header row and a total row.
- Click any Order Month cell and read the formula bar. It shows `=MONTH([@[Order Date]])`. If it shows `=MONTH(C7)`, the column was written with an address and objective 4.1.2 is unanswered.
- Click any Status cell and press `Shift+F3`. **Function Arguments** reopens on IFS with every box filled. If the whole formula is sitting inside the first box, it was typed as text and a grader that re-parses it will fail it.
- All thirty status cells are coloured and the colour matches the key. Change one destination from GUANAJUATO to VERACRUZ and the cell repaints from blue to green with nothing else touched. Change it back.
- The Order Month cells carry bars whose length tracks the month number, longest in June.
- The total row shows 30 under Status and a number between 100 and 500 under Quantity. Both formula bars start with `SUBTOTAL`. Filter the table to one product and both numbers move.
- **Banded Rows** and **Banded Columns** are both selected in **Table Style Options**.
- Click any Order Date cell. The formula bar shows a date, not `=RANDBETWEEN(...)`. Press `F9` several times and nothing moves.

## Known problems in the source

- The workbook ships largely finished. The table already exists, already carries Table Style Light 2, and is already named `Lorena`. Both calculated columns are written, and both conditional formats are in place. Working from the CSV files above gives back the exercise the instruction sheet actually describes.
- The shipped total row disagrees with the shipped instructions. It uses **Sum** on Quantity where task 10 asks for **Average**, and it adds a **Count** on Product that nothing asked for.
- **Banded Columns** is off in the shipped file, although the instructions ask for it.
- The helper parked in cell L13, `=RANDBETWEEN(44927,45105)`, stops at 28 June rather than the 30 June the instructions name. The correct upper bound is 45107.
- The Status formula is stored in the file as `_xlfn.IFS`. That prefix is how Excel writes a function that did not exist in the version the file claims to target, and it means the formula was written on a newer build than the workbook advertises. On Office 2019 and Microsoft 365 it opens and calculates normally. On anything older the whole column opens as `#NAME?` and retyping does not fix it, because the function is genuinely absent there. Check the build on the lab machines before class, and rewrite the column as a nested IF if they are older than 2019.
- The instruction sheet names the function as `SI.CONJUNTO (IFS)`, in a course that is otherwise English first. `SI.CONJUNTO` is what a Spanish interface accepts in the cell; `IFS` is what an English one accepts.
- Sheet `Ex1-2` lays its four columns out at B, E, F and G, which does not line up with the B, F, G, H the table uses. Pasting it in one block lands Product under Quantity. Paste the order numbers and the three text columns separately, or rearrange the block first.
